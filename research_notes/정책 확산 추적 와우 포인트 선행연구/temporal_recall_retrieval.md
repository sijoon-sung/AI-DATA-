# Temporal-aware, high-recall retrieval for finding the EARLIEST relevant evidence (RQ1)

Scope: the RQ1 "wow point" for the KISTI 2026 DATA·AI competition entry (Korean metropolitan council minutes, 2024-01 to 2025-12, 7,768 minutes, 3.56M passages of about 400 characters). Claim to test: top-k similarity RAG returns the MOST SIMILAR passages, not the EARLIEST, so it fails on "when/where first" questions. "First observation" is therefore a recall problem.

## Q1. Topic Detection and Tracking (TDT): first story detection and new event detection. Methods, metrics, and how "earliest mention of a known topic" differs

### Takeaway
TDT defined first story detection (FSD) as an online decision task: is this incoming story the first about a *previously unseen* event? It was scored with a detection cost (miss and false-alarm rates), and it was shown to be hard, with performance bounded by tracking quality. RQ1 asks a different question. It is retrospective and query-driven: given a known issue, find the minimum timestamp over the corpus's relevant set. Its accuracy is limited by recall at the temporal frontier, not by novelty scoring. TDT *tracking* (following an issue once seeded) maps directly onto the "follow-up flow to other councils" output.

### Inferences
- **Formal difference.** FSD asks, for each document d_t in the stream, whether its event is new given everything before it: an online binary decision about an *unknown* event. RQ1 asks, for a *known* issue q and each council c, for t*(q,c) = min{ t(p) : p ∈ R(q,c) }, where R is the relevant set. This is a retrospective **min-aggregation over a relevant set**. It is correct only if the earliest element of R is retrieved and verified. That makes it a frontier-recall problem: you need recall *at the earliest dates*, not recall overall.
- **Error asymmetry.** If verification is perfect, any retrieval miss can only make the estimate later (t̂ ≥ t*), a one-sided late bias. A false positive at an early date makes it too early, and the min operator lets a single false positive dominate. So the pipeline needs high recall in candidate generation and high precision in verification, specifically near the frontier. This two-sided sensitivity is a contribution worth stating. No reviewed paper formalizes it for RAG (see Gaps).
- The Allan et al. (2000) "bounded by tracking" result suggests an RQ1 analogue: first-observation accuracy ≤ P(earliest relevant passage ∈ candidate set) × P(verifier accepts it and rejects earlier non-relevant ones).
- TDT tracking (given a few seed stories, find subsequent on-topic stories) is the right framing for the *follow-up flow to other councils*. TDT-style cost metrics (miss and false-alarm rates per council) can score the diffusion table.

### Gaps
- Fiscus & Doddington (2002), the original TDT evaluation chapter, and the exact TDT cost parameters (C_Miss, C_FA, P_Target) were not fetched. The formula is from a secondary source.
- The full text of Allan et al. (2000) was unreadable (garbled PDF), so its quantitative bounds are not reported.
- No neural FSD work from 2024–2026 was found. Search budget ran out before a dedicated search.

## Q2. High-recall retrieval / technology-assisted review (TAR): what to borrow for estimating and guaranteeing recall of early mentions

### Takeaway
TAR offers a mature toolkit: continuous active learning (CAL), stopping rules (knee, target, quantile and hypergeometric tests, Chao's estimator, RL stopping), and sample-based recall certification with confidence bounds. These transfer to RQ1 if the target is redefined from "recall of all relevant documents" to **"no relevant passage exists before the reported first date"**, i.e., prefix (pre-frontier) recall. Two 2026 SIGIR and WWW papers already argue that stopping should depend on the decision being made rather than a fixed recall target. "Is the earliest date certain?" is exactly such a decision-based stopping criterion.

- **[V] ADOPT**: Cormack, G. V., & Grossman, M. R. (2014). Evaluation of machine-learning protocols for technology-assisted review in electronic discovery. *SIGIR 2014*. DOI 10.1145/2600428.2609601. What: on 8 review tasks (4 from the TREC 2009 Legal Track, 4 from real legal matters), non-random training with keyword-seeded **continuous active learning (CAL)** needed significantly less review effort (P<0.01) for any recall level than passive learning, and CAL outperformed uncertainty-sampling active learning. Relation: the method to adopt for the pre-frontier audit. Seed with verified mentions, then iteratively review the top-scored unreviewed passages dated *before* the current first date. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1145/2600428.2609601)
- **[V-meta] ADOPT**: Cormack, G. V., & Grossman, M. R. (2015). Autonomy and Reliability of Continuous Active Learning for Technology-Assisted Review. arXiv:1504.06868. What: makes CAL autonomous by eliminating topic-specific parameters (AutoTAR). This was the baseline model implementation for the TREC 2015 Total Recall track (per the 2016 paper's abstract). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1504.06868)

### Inferences
- **Prefix-recall reformulation (core adaptation).** For RQ1 we do not need total recall. We need the reported first passage p̂ (date t̂) to be relevant, *and* the pre-frontier set S_<t̂ = {p : t(p) < t̂, council = c} to contain no relevant passage. S_<t̂ is usually much smaller than the corpus (early months only), and it shrinks as t̂ moves earlier. Exhaustive or near-exhaustive review is therefore cheapest exactly where it matters. This is the RQ1-specific cost advantage over standard TAR.
- **Certificate.** Stratify S_<t̂ by reranker or hybrid score. Review the high-score strata exhaustively and draw random samples from the low-score strata. If none of n random draws from a stratum is relevant, the one-sided 95% upper bound on that stratum's prevalence is about 3/n, since (1−p)^n = 0.05 gives p ≈ 3/n. This is a binomial derivation, not a cited result. Multiply by stratum size to bound the expected number of missed earlier mentions. Callaghan & Müller-Hansen's hypergeometric test gives an exact finite-population version.
- **Low-prevalence warning.** Cormack & Grossman (2016) note that reliability is costly when relevant documents are few. Early mentions of an emerging issue are by definition few. Report a confidence flag per (issue, council) rather than claiming guaranteed recall everywhere.
- CAL fits naturally: seed with verified mentions, train a lightweight logistic regression on frozen bge-m3 embeddings (CPU-cheap), and re-score only S_<t̂ until a batch yields no new relevant passages and the sampling test passes.

### Gaps
- The exact knee-method and target-method parameters from Cormack & Grossman (2016) could not be verified (the PDF was not machine-readable here; RLStop only describes them qualitatively). Do not quote parameter values without checking the paper.
- No TAR paper was found that targets an *order statistic* (earliest relevant document) rather than recall of the full set. This absence supports novelty but was not exhaustively searched.
- The SIGIR 2025 "Generalised and Adaptable RL Stopping Method" (Bin-Hezam & Stevenson) appeared only in a search summary and was not verified.

## Q3. Time-aware / temporal RAG and temporal QA (2021–2026): is there work on retrieving the EARLIEST relevant document or on "first mention" questions? How badly do standard RAG systems fail?

### Takeaway
Temporal QA and RAG research is very active (TimeQA, SituatedQA, RealTime QA, TS-Retriever, TempRALM, MRAG/TempRAGEval, ChronoQA, TA-RAG, TempRetriever, TimelyRAG, the ACL 2026 survey). All of it shows that similarity-only retrievers ignore temporal constraints, and the measured losses are large. However, almost all of it targets **"what was true at/before/after time T"** (temporal constraint satisfaction) or **recency/version selection**. None of it targets **"at what time did X first appear in this corpus"** (an order statistic over the corpus's own timestamps). The closest are MRAG (its benchmark perturbs questions with "first/last" implicit conditions), TA-RAG (coverage of a whole period for diachronic questions), ChronoQA's aggregate type, and ordinal questions in TempQuestions. No paper isolating "earliest mention" as a RAG task was found.

### Inferences
- **Novelty gap (as far as searched).** Temporal RAG work optimizes *temporal constraint satisfaction* (as of/before/after T), *recency or version*, or *period coverage*. None optimizes *the earliest element of the relevant set*, and none reports first-mention accuracy or a recall certificate for it. MRAG's "first/last" perturbations are the nearest, but they operate over facts with explicit in-text dates in Wikipedia.
- **Why similarity ≠ earliest (testable hypotheses for the paper).** (i) **Base-rate effect.** If an issue has |R| relevant passages and rank is independent of time, P(earliest ∈ top-k) ≈ k/|R|. For example, k=10 and |R|=300 gives about 3%. (ii) **Burst effect.** Session months dominate (1,112 minutes in Nov 2024 vs 64 in Jan 2025), so top-k concentrates in dense months. (iii) **Maturity effect.** First mentions tend to be brief, passing remarks (5-minute free speeches, side questions in budget review) with lower similarity than later, fully developed agenda debates, so the earliest is systematically ranked low. Effect (iii) is a hypothesis that should be measured, not asserted.
- ChronoQA's result that a naive temporal filter *hurt* multi-document recall, and TA-RAG's result that a general reranker *hurt* accuracy, both warn that adding time naively can backfire. Ablations should be reported.
- In council minutes the passage timestamp is the meeting date, which is reliable, unlike news, where publication time ≠ event time. RQ1 measures *first mention in the minutes*, not the real-world origin of a policy. The paper should state this clearly and treat in-text back-references (e.g., "지난 회기에...") as evidence of left-censoring.

### Gaps
- The search found no paper defining "earliest relevant document retrieval" or "first-mention QA" in RAG. Absence of evidence is limited by search budget: the web-search quota ran out and no further targeted queries were possible.
- The quantitative failure numbers are from other domains (Wikipedia, Chinese news, finance news). No Korean or government-minutes temporal RAG benchmark was found.
- The final venues of RealTime QA, LOTUS, ArchivalQA (SIGIR 2022?) and TempRetriever (2026 ACM conference) were not confirmed.

## Q4. Hybrid sparse+dense retrieval in Korean / multilingual settings (bge-m3), Korean benchmarks, and reranker verification costs

### Takeaway
bge-m3 is the right backbone. On MIRACL-Korean, dense alone reaches nDCG@10 69.9, sparse 61.5, dense+sparse 71.2 and all three modes 72.1, versus BM25 at 37.1. That shows hybrid helps, and that a naive BM25 baseline for Korean is weak unless properly tokenized. Korean fine-tunes of bge-m3 (KURE-v1) give only small average gains. bge-reranker-v2-m3 (0.6B, multilingual, fp16) is affordable for tens of thousands of pairs per issue but not for 3.56M. So recall must come from cheap first-stage retrieval designed for *temporal coverage*, followed by reranking and LLM verification of a bounded candidate set.

- **[V] ADOPT (backbone)**: Chen, J., Xiao, S., Zhang, P., Luo, K., Lian, D., & Liu, Z. (2024). ### Inferences
- **Cost arithmetic (estimates, to be benchmarked on the actual GPU).** Reranking all 3.56M passages per issue is infeasible. At an assumed few hundred pairs per second for a 0.6B fp16 cross-encoder on 400-character inputs, it would take hours per issue. A candidate set of 5k–20k passages per issue is feasible in minutes. LLM verification with Qwen2.5-7B 4-bit is the scarcest resource, likely on the order of 1 passage per second or less with short JSON outputs. Spend it only on reranker-positive candidates in **chronological order** plus the audit samples.
- **Index once, filter by metadata.** Precompute bge-m3 dense vectors (1024-d; about 7.3 GB in fp16 for 3.56M vectors, so keep them on disk or CPU with FAISS) and sparse lexical weights once. Then run **per-(council, month) filtered retrieval**: 17 councils × 24 months = 408 buckets, top-n per bucket. This guarantees temporal coverage that global top-k structurally lacks; it is the TA-RAG anchor idea implemented by metadata filtering.
- The MIRACL-ko numbers suggest bge-m3 sparse is a reasonable lexical channel in Korean. A morphologically tokenized BM25 (Kiwi or Mecab) may still add recall for rare proper nouns and ordinance names. This is not verified for this corpus (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Korean BM25 with morphological analyzers vs bge-m3 sparse was not researched: the web-search quota was exhausted before this query.
- No published throughput numbers were found for bge-reranker-v2-m3 or Qwen2.5-7B-4bit on 8GB GPUs. Measure them locally.
- No Korean benchmark exists for government or council minutes retrieval. The closest domain set is AutoRAGRetrieval (public and legal PDFs) inside Korean MTEB.

## Q5. Evaluation: first-mention accuracy, date error and retrieval recall when exhaustive labels are impossible

### Takeaway
Combine three established IR evaluation practices: pooling across diverse systems, inferred and sampled metrics for incomplete judgments, and sampling-based recall confidence intervals. Add LLM-assisted relevance labeling validated against humans, and score dates with tolerance-aware and interval metrics. Treat the first observation as an **interval-censored** quantity (Turnbull-style), and flag left-censoring at the data start.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] ADOPT (pool bias)**: Zobel, J. (1998). How reliable are the results of large-scale information retrieval experiments? *SIGIR 1998*. DOI 10.1145/290941.291014. What: examines whether TREC pooling yields trustworthy, fair measurements. Relation: our gold first mentions must not be built only from our own system's candidates, or the evaluation is biased toward it. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1145/290941.291014)
- **[V] ADOPT**: Buckley, C., & Voorhees, E. M. (2004). Retrieval evaluation with incomplete information. *SIGIR 2004*. DOI 10.1145/1008992.1009000. What: standard measures are not robust to substantially incomplete judgments, so the paper introduces bpref. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1145/1008992.1009000)

### Inferences (proposed evaluation protocol)
- **Gold set, tier A: lexically anchored issues (near-exhaustive truth).** Choose 15–30 issues with distinctive surface forms: named ordinances, program names or coinages (e.g., 기후동행카드, 늘봄학교, 출산지원금 variants, 고향사랑기부제). Regex and morphology search over all 3.56M passages, followed by manual or LLM-checked verification, gives near-complete R(q,c) and an exact t*(q,c). This tier measures **how often vanilla top-k RAG misses the true first meeting**, with little label uncertainty. It is the cleanest test of the RQ1 claim.
- **Gold set, tier B: paraphrastic issues (pooled and sampled truth).** For 15–30 conceptual issues (e.g., 청년 유출, 저출생 대응, 폭염 대책), pool candidates from many systems: dense, sparse, hybrid, time-stratified, LLM-expanded queries, and keyword lists. Judge the pool with Qwen plus a human audit sample. Then draw a **random sample of unpooled pre-frontier passages** per council to estimate residual missed early mentions, with a Webber-style CI or a hypergeometric test.
- **Metrics.** (1) First-Hit@meeting: exact match of the first meeting per (issue, council). (2) Signed date error in days and in *council sessions*, reporting the late-bias rate P(t̂ > t*) separately from the early-error rate P(t̂ < t*). (3) Interval coverage: P(t* ∈ reported interval) and mean interval width. (4) Prefix recall: fraction of gold relevant passages dated ≤ t* + Δ that were retrieved. (5) Diffusion-order agreement: Kendall's τ between predicted and gold council adoption orders, and C_Det-style miss/false-alarm rates on "council c mentioned issue q by date T". (6) Cost: GPU-minutes and number of LLM verifications per issue.
- **Baselines and ablations.** (a) Vanilla top-k RAG (k = 5/10/20), where Qwen answers "언제 처음 언급되었나?" from the top-k. (b) Top-k plus sorting retrieved passages by date. (c) Hybrid dense+sparse top-K (K=100/1000) plus reranker. (d) The proposed approach (time-stratified union, reranking, chronological LLM verification, pre-frontier audit). Ablate time stratification, query expansion, the audit, and reranker thresholds.
- **Censoring.** Mark first observations as left-censored when t̂ falls within the first observed session of that council (or within roughly the first 2 months of 2024-01), or when the first passage refers back to earlier discussion (e.g., "지난번에도 말씀드렸듯이"). Mark them as interval-censored otherwise, with the interval being (previous screened meeting of council c, t̂]. Report a separate "no mention through 2025-12" right-censored category for diffusion analysis.

### Gaps
- No published metric specifically for "first-occurrence date accuracy" was found. The metrics above are assembled from TDT, TLS, TAR and survival-analysis practice. Present them as a proposed protocol.
- Agreement between Qwen2.5-7B (4-bit) and human relevance labels on Korean council text is unknown and must be measured.

## Synthesis: (a) novelty assessment, (b) feasible methods and evaluation on an 8GB GPU, (c) wow-point framing

### Takeaway
RQ1 is **moderately novel as a framing and strongly defensible as an empirical contribution**. Its components are all established: TDT, TAR recall certification, temporal RAG, hybrid retrieval and interval censoring. The search found no work that (i) defines corpus-level *first observation* as an order-statistic retrieval problem for RAG, (ii) quantifies vanilla top-k RAG's late bias on it, and (iii) reports first observations as censoring-aware intervals with a recall certificate. The closest prior works are MRAG/TempRAGEval (EMNLP Findings 2025), TA-RAG (arXiv 2025), ChronoQA (Sci Data 2025), TDT FSD and retrospective detection (1998–2000), and Cormack & Grossman (2014/2016) plus the 2026 decision-centric stopping papers.

### Cited Findings
- Closest prior work and what separates RQ1 from each:
  - MRAG/TempRAGEval handles "first/last" *constraints over in-text fact dates* in Wikipedia. RQ1 is the minimum over *document timestamps* of an unknown-size relevant set, with no gold list of candidate facts. — [MRAG](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.findings-emnlp.167)
  - TA-RAG retrieves contiguous coverage over a time window for analytical questions. It has no "earliest" target, no certificate, and its general reranker hurt accuracy. — [TA-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22917v1)
  - ChronoQA shows multi-document recall@5 of 0.27 vs 0.71 for single-document questions. Its aggregate questions are pairwise comparisons, not corpus-wide earliest. — [ChronoQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12282)
  - TDT FSD works online on *unknown* events, and its accuracy is bounded by tracking. RQ1 is offline on a *known* issue, and its accuracy is bounded by frontier recall. — [Allan et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/354756.354843); [TDT pilot](https://doi.org/10.1184/r1/6626252.v1)
  - TAR certifies recall of *all* relevant documents. RQ1 only needs *pre-frontier* recall, which is cheaper and decision-centric, in line with Fletcher & Stevenson (2026). — [Cormack & Grossman 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2911451.2911510); [Fletcher & Stevenson 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3805712.3809548)
  - Policy-text tracing (Wilkerson et al. 2015) relies on verbatim reuse in bills. RQ1 handles paraphrastic spoken deliberation across 17 councils. — [Wilkerson](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12175)

### Inferences

**(a) Novelty assessment.**
- *Novel enough to headline:* the claim and evidence that **"most similar ≠ earliest"**, with a measured, one-sided late bias of vanilla top-k RAG on first-observation questions over a real, bursty, multi-source government corpus. Also novel: **prefix-recall certification** (TAR statistics applied only to the pre-frontier set) and **censoring-aware interval reporting** for first observation in RAG outputs.
- *Not novel (cite, don't claim):* that dense retrievers ignore temporal constraints (TS-Retriever, MRAG, TempRALM, ChronoQA), that hybrid dense+sparse beats either alone (bge-m3), that CAL and sample-based stopping give high recall (Cormack & Grossman; Callaghan; Lewis et al.), and that RAG fails on corpus-global questions (GraphRAG).
- *Risk:* reviewers may call the result obvious ("of course top-k misses the minimum"). Pre-empt this with the base-rate, burst and maturity decomposition, plus the measured magnitude on tier-A anchored issues, and by showing that *simple fixes* (sorting top-k by date, larger k, naive date filters) still fail or are costly. ChronoQA and TA-RAG already show that naive temporal filters and rerankers can hurt.

**(b) Concrete pipeline, feasible on an 8GB GPU (bge-m3 + bge-reranker-v2-m3 + Qwen2.5-7B-4bit; run one model on the GPU at a time).**
1. **Offline indexing (once).** bge-m3 dense vectors (FAISS on CPU/disk; IVF-PQ if RAM is tight) and sparse lexical weights (inverted index) for 3.56M passages. Add metadata: council, meeting ID, date, session, and speaker role if available.
2. **Issue query set.** Seed description, then LLM-generated paraphrases and Korean synonym variants (Petrović 2012 motivation), plus 2–5 verified seed passages from any period. Keep the issue definition fixed and written down; first-observation results are only as good as the definition.
3. **High-recall candidate generation (union, no reranking yet).** (a) Global dense top-2,000 and sparse top-2,000 per query variant. (b) **Time-stratified retrieval**: top-10 to 20 per (council × month) bucket under dense+sparse fusion (408 buckets, about 4k–8k passages). (c) Exact or regex lexical anchors. (d) Neighbour expansion: ±1 passage around hits, because first mentions are often split across chunk boundaries.
4. **Rerank** the union (about 10k–20k pairs per issue) with bge-reranker-v2-m3 in fp16 at max_length 512. Calibrate a threshold on tier-A/B dev labels for high recall (e.g., ≥ 0.95 recall of dev positives).
5. **Chronological verification.** Per council, sort reranker-positive candidates by date ascending. Qwen verifies each against a strict rubric (is the issue *substantively* raised, not just a keyword?) and extracts a verbatim evidence span in JSON. The first verified passage gives t̂_c. Also verify the next 2–3 earliest candidates as a guard against false-positive dominance.
6. **Pre-frontier audit (certificate).** For S_<t̂_c, run CAL-lite: logistic regression on frozen bge-m3 embeddings with verified labels, re-score S_<t̂_c, and review top batches until a batch yields zero positives. Then draw a random sample from the remaining low-score strata and apply a hypergeometric or binomial test (Callaghan & Müller-Hansen; Lewis et al. 2021). Output "certified at 95%" or "low confidence" per (issue, council).
7. **Report** per issue: the earliest council and meeting (with verbatim quote), the interval (last screened meeting without mention, first mention], a left-censoring flag, and follow-up councils in order with intervals and quotes. Aggregate onset distributions with Turnbull-style interval-censored estimates if diffusion curves are needed.
8. **Evaluation.** Tier-A anchored issues (near-exhaustive truth) and tier-B pooled-and-sampled issues. Metrics: First-Hit@meeting, signed date error (days and sessions), late-bias rate, interval coverage and width, prefix recall, Kendall τ of council order, and GPU-minutes per issue. Baselines: vanilla top-k RAG (k=5/10/20), top-k + date sort, hybrid+rerank top-K, and the full pipeline with ablations.

**(c) Suggested wow-point framing (Korean/English).**
- 헤드라인 (headline): **"가장 비슷한 문서 ≠ 가장 이른 문서: '언제·어디서 처음'은 검색 정확도가 아니라 재현율(recall)의 문제다."** In English: "The most similar passage is not the earliest one: first observation is a recall problem, not a similarity problem."
- One-figure story: on anchored issues, vanilla top-10 RAG's reported first date versus the true first meeting. Show the late-bias distribution in sessions or months, and how much it shrinks with time-stratified, high-recall retrieval plus chronological verification. Annotate the Nov-2024 burst to show *why* top-k gravitates there.
- Second point: **"최초 관측 = 구간 + 신뢰도"** ("first observation = interval + confidence"). Every first observation is reported as (last meeting without mention, first mention] with a recall certificate and a left-censoring flag. This turns a fragile point estimate into an auditable, policy-grade claim, which is attractive to the National Assembly Library as a user.
- Positioning sentence for the report: "Prior temporal RAG work makes retrievers *respect* time constraints (MRAG, TS-Retriever, TA-RAG). We show that *finding the first* requires a different objective, the minimum over the relevant set. We borrow TDT's first-story lens and TAR's recall certification to make it reliable on 3.56M Korean council passages with an 8GB GPU."

### Gaps
- The novelty claim rests on searches across TDT, TAR and temporal RAG (2021–2026). The web-search quota was exhausted before searching (i) Korean-language literature on 지방의회 회의록 retrieval or issue tracking, (ii) "origin tracing / provenance retrieval" for claims, and (iii) Korean BM25 morphology effects. A reviewer could surface work in those areas.
- The MRAG, TA-RAG and ChronoQA magnitudes come from other languages and domains. RQ1's actual late-bias magnitude on council minutes is unknown until measured.
- The cost and throughput numbers above are engineering estimates, not cited results. Benchmark them on the target 8GB GPU before quoting them.
