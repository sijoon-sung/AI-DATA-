## Q1. Calibration for rare-event binary forecasts (reliability diagrams, Brier decomposition, ECE pitfalls, Platt/isotonic/beta, temporal CV, few events)

### Takeaway
For a ~1.4% base-rate problem, "Brier no better than base rate" is an expected symptom of a calibration (reliability) term that eats the small resolution an AUC≈0.72 model has; the remedy is out-of-time recalibration with a low-variance parametric map (intercept-only, Platt/logistic, or beta) fitted on a pre-origin calibration slice, evaluated with proper scores (Brier skill score, log loss), weak-calibration measures (calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope), and stable reliability diagrams (CORP or smooth ECE) rather than binned ECE or Hosmer–Lemeshow. With ~300 events per fold you can estimate weak calibration well per fold, but flexible calibration curves and grade-level hit rates should be pooled across folds.

- **Murphy, A. H. (1973). ### Inferences
- With base rate p≈0.014, the climatological Brier score is p(1−p)≈0.0138. Murphy: BS = REL − RES + UNC with UNC≈0.0138, so any REL larger than the (small) RES yields negative skill even with AUC≈0.72. The poor Brier is therefore almost certainly a calibration-in-the-large/slope problem, not a lack of signal — a point worth stating explicitly in the J2 narrative.
- Likely causes to check (in order): (i) class weighting/resampling (van den Goorbergh; King & Zeng); (ii) base-rate drift between training origins and test windows (Van Calster 2019 "population drift") — e.g., council-term cycles after local elections (June 2018, June 2022) may shift discussion rates [domain inference, verify on your data]; (iii) overfitting (calibration slope < 1).
- Recommended calibrator: fit on out-of-time predictions from the most recent pre-origin window (e.g., train on origins ≤ t−12m, calibrate on the window t−12m→t−6m whose labels are fully observed by t), then refit the base model on all data ≤ t−6m-labelled and apply the calibrator. Prefer intercept-only → Platt → beta in increasing complexity; choose by log loss on an inner time split; never isotonic as the production map with <~2,000 calibration cases.
- Report per fold: Brier, Brier Skill Score vs pre-origin base rate, log loss, O/E ratio, calibration intercept & slope (with CIs), AUC and PR-AUC; pooled across folds: CORP reliability diagram with bands + bias-corrected Murphy decomposition.

## Q2. Rolling-origin / time-series cross-validation and data-leakage taxonomy (incl. leakage via preprocessing or issue/label definitions built on full data)

### Takeaway
The leakage literature gives a ready-made checklist for J2: Kapoor & Narayanan's eight-type taxonomy (especially L1.2 pre-processing on train+test, L1.3 feature selection on train+test, L3.1 temporal leakage) maps one-to-one onto risks in this project (issue list/topic model built on the full corpus, vocabulary/IDF, scalers, thresholds). For non-stationary data, repeated out-of-sample (rolling-origin) evaluation is the empirically most accurate estimator; random k-fold CV is only justified for purely autoregressive stationary setups. Their civil-war case study (rare political events: leakage made RF/AdaBoost look better than logistic regression) is a directly analogous warning for our LightGBM-vs-LR comparison.

### Cited Findings
- **Kapoor, S., & Narayanan, A. (2023). Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science. *Patterns*, 4(9), 100804.** — leakage found in 17 fields affecting 294 papers; taxonomy of eight types: L1.1 no test set, L1.2 pre-processing on training and test set, L1.3 feature selection on training and test set, L1.4 duplicates, L2 illegitimate features, L3.1 temporal leakage, L3.2 non-independence between train and test, L3.3 sampling bias in test distribution; proposes a 21-question "model info sheet" arguing clean separation (L1), legitimate features (L2), and test set from the distribution of interest (L3). Civil-war prediction case study: of 12 reproducible papers, the 4 claiming complex ML (RF, AdaBoost) beats logistic regression all had leakage errors; after correction complex models were no better than LR (one AUC gap fell 0.14→0.01). **Relation:** adopt the taxonomy and model info sheet as the "leakage audit card"; pitfall/differentiation — show LightGBM's gain over LR survives a leakage-free protocol (or honestly report it does not). **[V]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856); [project site](https://reproducible.cs.princeton.edu/)
- **Kapoor, S., Cantrell, E. M., Peng, K., Pham, T. H., Bail, C. A., Gundersen, O. E., Hofman, J. M., Hullman, J., Lones, M. A., Malik, M. M., Nanayakkara, P., Poldrack, R. A., Raji, I. - **Paleka, D., Goel, S., Geiping, J., & Tramèr, F. (2025/2026). Pitfalls in evaluating language model forecasters. arXiv 2506.00723; *ICLR 2026*.** — (also central to Q3) identifies *logical leakage* in backtests: selecting questions because they have resolved in the present can imply the answer; recommends evaluating across multiple disjoint periods and reporting Brier, calibration and log score; notes Brier scores are not comparable across base rates. **Relation:** pitfall — building the *issue list* from issues known (or known to have diffused) by 2025 is logical leakage; issue lists must be frozen per origin. **[V]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006489)

### Inferences
- Map of project-specific leakage risks to Kapoor's taxonomy (use this as the "leakage audit card"):
  - L1.2/L1.3 — topic model / keyword dictionary / issue clustering, IDF weights, scalers, feature selection, hyperparameter search fitted on the full 20xx–2025 corpus → refit per origin using only minutes dated ≤ origin.
  - L3.1 — labels or features using minutes after the origin (e.g., "number of adopters" counted through end of 2025); training instances whose 6-month outcome window extends past the origin (need a purge/embargo of the last 6 months of training origins — standard practice; no citation verified).
  - L3.3 / Paleka "logical leakage" — risk set containing only issues that eventually diffused (survivorship); issue definitions refined after seeing which issues spread; dropping councils/issues with sparse data based on full-period counts.
  - L2 — features that are proxies of the outcome (e.g., mentions of the issue in the same council during the outcome window, or committee-agenda items that are logged before but published after the origin).
  - Calibrator and verbal-grade thresholds are also "models": fitting them on test folds is L1.2-type leakage.
- A simple "leakage canary" test strengthens J2: re-run the pipeline with the issue list/topic model built on the full corpus and show the resulting AUC inflation versus the frozen-per-origin pipeline (the Kapoor civil-war logic in miniature). If inflation is visible, it demonstrates the design choice matters; if not, it shows robustness.
- Rolling origins every 6 months with 6-month horizons give non-overlapping test windows (disjoint periods, per Paleka) and ~300 events each; report per-origin metrics, not only pooled metrics, to expose drift.

### Gaps
- No verified source specifically on leakage from *topic models / issue dictionaries* fitted on the full text corpus in political-text forecasting; the argument rests on Kapoor's L1.2/L1.3 plus Paleka's logical leakage.
- "Purged/embargoed CV" (López de Prado) is the usual finance reference for overlapping label windows — [U], not verified this session.

## Q3. LLM look-ahead bias and knowledge-cutoff leakage in forecasting evaluation

### Takeaway
LLM-based forecasts evaluated on periods inside the model's training data are not identifiable as skill vs memorization: models reproduce pre-cutoff facts, prompting "pretend it is year X" or masking names/dates does not stop recall, and stated knowledge cutoffs are unreliable. The cleanest fix used by benchmarks is to evaluate only on outcomes unknowable at the model's training time (ForecastBench, MIRAI dynamic splits) or to use chronologically consistent models. Our design sidesteps the core problem by never letting the LLM produce probabilities (it only verbalizes model outputs from pre-origin evidence), but explanations in backtests can still leak post-origin knowledge and must be audited.

### Cited Findings
- **Sarkar, S., & Vafa, K. (2024). Lookahead bias in pretrained language models. Working paper (SSRN/NBER-hosted); poster at the ICML 2025 Workshop "2nd Workshop on Reliable and Responsible Foundation Models".** — defines lookahead bias (training text contains outcomes an analysis restricted to past information should not see); tests based on events that should be unpredictable from a pre-specified information set detect it in predicting risk factors from earnings calls (Llama-2) and election winners from candidate biographies; prompt-based workarounds are insufficient; remedy is models trained only on text prior to the analysis period. **Relation:** pitfall/differentiation — justifies the rule "LLM never forecasts"; probabilities come from a model trained on pre-origin data. **[V]** — [ICML 2025 workshop page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/48685); [NBER-hosted PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f205530/f205530.pdf)
- **Karger, E., et al. (incl. P. E. Tetlock) (2025). ForecastBench: A dynamic benchmark of AI forecasting capabilities. *ICLR 2025*.** arXiv 2409.19839 — 1,000 automatically generated, regularly updated questions about future events with no known answer at submission, so resolutions cannot be in training data by construction; on 200 questions, expert forecasters beat the best LLM (p<0.001). **Relation:** differentiation — the "unknown-at-submission" principle motivates pre-registering (hashing) our 2026-H1 forecasts so they can be scored later. **[V]** — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea74e45a229dac70b5b63b28d8934db6-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Paleka, D., Goel, S., Geiping, J., & Tramèr, F. (2025). Pitfalls in evaluating language model forecasters. arXiv 2506.00723; *ICLR 2026*.** — temporal leakage forms: logical leakage (question selection reveals outcome), unreliable date-restricted retrieval (mis-dated pages, sidebars/comments reflecting present knowledge, ranking algorithms that "know" the future), knowledge-cutoff uncertainty ("cutoffs are more of a guideline than a guarantee"); recommends a few-month buffer around cutoffs, curated time-indexed corpora or simple retrieval (e.g., TF-IDF), multiple disjoint backtest periods, and reporting at least Brier, calibration and log score; Brier not comparable across base rates. **Relation:** adopt — use only our own time-stamped minutes corpus (no web search) for retrieval; apply the buffer logic to Qwen2.5. **[V]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006489)
- **Halawi, D., Zhang, F., Chen, Y.-H., & Steinhardt, J. (2024). ## Q4. Verbal probability expressions and grade communication (IPCC calibrated language, Kent's WEP, Dhami & Mandel; verifiable grade definitions)

### Takeaway
Verbal probability words are interpreted very inconsistently (and regress toward 50%), and coarsening numeric forecasts into verbal categories measurably loses accuracy; the best-supported practice is a fixed lexicon where each term is always printed together with its numeric range in brackets. For our rare-event setting, conventional lexicons (IPCC "likely" = 66–100%; ICD 203 "likely" = 55–80%) are actively misleading, because even our top grade will have hit rates near 10%; grades should therefore be named as attention/alert tiers whose meaning is an *empirically observed backtest frequency* (with CI and lift over base rate), not a probability adjective.

### Cited Findings
- **Kent, S. (1964). Words of estimative probability. *Studies in Intelligence*, 8(4).** — contrasted "poets" (verbal) and "mathematicians" (odds); proposed mapping estimative terms to odds so readers can distinguish fact from judgment and the degree of certitude; not adopted by CIA at the time; lexicons proliferated after IC analytic standards (2007). **Relation:** adopt as the historical anchor for "words must map to numbers". **[V]** — [CIA CSI page](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/archives/vol-8-no-4/words-of-estimative-probability); [PDF](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Words-of-Estimative-Probability.pdf)
- **Mastrandrea, M. D., Field, C. B., Stocker, T. F., Edenhofer, O., Ebi, K. L., Frame, D. J., Held, H., Kriegler, E., Mach, K. J., Matschoss, P. R., Plattner, G.-K., Yohe, G. W., & Zwiers, F. W. (2010). Guidance note for lead authors of the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report on consistent treatment of uncertainties. IPCC.** — likelihood scale: virtually certain 99–100%, very likely 90–100%, likely 66–100%, about as likely as not 33–66%, unlikely 0–33%, very unlikely 0–10%, exceptionally unlikely 0–1% (plus extremely likely 95–100%, more likely than not >50–100%, extremely unlikely 0–5% when appropriate); confidence (very low…very high, based on evidence and agreement) is a separate, non-probabilistic qualifier. **Relation:** adopt the *two-axis* idea (likelihood vs confidence/evidence) — our "evidence strength" can be shown separately from probability; pitfall — the IPCC words are inappropriate labels for 1–15% events. **[V]** for document/core writing team (full author list partly [U]); table **[V-sec]** via reproduction — [IPCC PDF](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf); [SIPTA slides reproducing the table](https://sipta.org/events/sipta-seminars/SIPTA-seminar-Brian_Hill.pdf)
- **Budescu, D. V., Broomell, S., & Por, H.-H. (2009). - **Dhami, M. K., & Mandel, D. R. (2022). - **Wintle, B. C., Fraser, H., Wills, B. C., Nicholson, A. E., & Fidler, F. (2019). - **Friedman, J. A., Baker, J. D., Mellers, B. A., Tetlock, P. E., & Zeckhauser, R. (2018). - **Mandel & Barnes (2014), *PNAS*** (see Q1) — intelligence analysts' verbal/numeric forecasts can be scored for calibration and recalibrated. **[V]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4121776/)

### Inferences
- Conventional lexicons fail for rare events: a pair with calibrated p=0.10 is "very unlikely" (IPCC 0–10%; ICD 203 5–20%) yet is ~7× the base rate and among the most actionable forecasts. Using "가능성 높음" for it would be read as ≥55–66% (and Budescu shows readers already distort words). Therefore name grades as *relative alert tiers* (e.g., 확산 주의 상/중/하 or "강한 확산 신호 / 보통 / 약함"), and define each by (i) an absolute frequency with CI and (ii) lift over base rate, printed in brackets every time (Wintle).
- "Verifiable grade" = a grade whose definition is a falsifiable statement: "Among (issue, council) pairs graded H at origins 2021–2025, X% [95% CI a–b] adopted within 6 months (base rate 1.4%)." This turns the IPCC/ICD idea of conventional mapping into an *empirically calibrated* mapping — a genuine differentiation.
- Keep Friedman's lesson: score the probabilities, not the grades; show that grade coarsening costs little (report Brier of probabilities vs Brier of grade-mean probabilities).

## Q5. Faithfulness and pitfalls of SHAP explanations; explanation evaluation; event-history/hazard interpretability

### Takeaway
TreeSHAP gives exact Shapley values for tree ensembles in polynomial time, but "which Shapley value" matters: interventional (marginal) expectations are "true to the model" and are the causally correct notion of dropping a feature (Janzing et al.), while observational/conditional or path-dependent values are "true to the data"; with correlated count features (likely here), attributions can be split arbitrarily, and impossibility results show complete-and-linear attributions can be no better than random for some end-tasks. Practical answer: define the end-task (explain *this* forecast's drivers), use interventional TreeSHAP with a pre-origin background, group correlated features, test faithfulness by perturbation, and — given LR performs similarly — consider the interpretable model as the primary explainer (Rudin).

### Cited Findings
- **Lundberg, S. M., Erion, G., Chen, H., DeGrave, A., Prutkin, J. M., Nair, B., Katz, R., Himmelfarb, J., Bansal, N., & Lee, S.-I. (2020). From local explanations to global understanding with explainable AI for trees. *Nature Machine Intelligence*, 2(1), 56–67.** — TreeExplainer (TreeSHAP): first polynomial-time exact Shapley explanations for trees; SHAP interaction values; global understanding by aggregating local explanations. **Relation:** adopt (method), with the caveats below. **[V]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7326367); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32607472/)
- **Janzing, D., Minorics, L., & Blöbaum, P. (2020). Feature relevance quantification in explainable AI: A causal problem. *AISTATS 2020*, PMLR 108:2907–2916.** — disagreement over Shapley value functions stems from conflating observational and interventional conditionals; argues unconditional (interventional) expectations are the right notion of "dropping" a feature. **Relation:** adopt — use `feature_perturbation="interventional"` TreeSHAP with a background sample from pre-origin training data. **[V]** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/janzing20a.html)
- **Chen, H., Janizek, J. D., Lundberg, S., & Lee, S.-I. (2020). True to the model or true to the data? arXiv 2006.16234.** — the choice between interventional and observational Shapley values is application-dependent: true-to-model (what the model uses) vs true-to-data (what the data says). **Relation:** adopt as explicit stance: our explanations are "true to the model" (why the model gave this probability), not causal claims about diffusion. **[V]** (preprint) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16234v1)
- **Aas, K., Jullum, M., & Løland, A. (2021). Explaining individual predictions when features are dependent: More accurate approximations to Shapley values. *Artificial Intelligence*, 298, 103502.** — feature-independence assumptions produce misleading Shapley values with correlated features, even for linear models; proposes dependence-aware estimation and *group-level* Shapley values for dependent feature groups. **Relation:** adopt grouping (e.g., "prior adoption pressure" = neighbor adopters + total adopters; "local salience" = mention counts) to avoid arbitrary credit splits among correlated counts. **[V]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10464v3); [UiO PDF](https://uio.no/studier/emner/matnat/math/STK9200/h21/aas_jullum_loeland2021.pdf)
- **Kumar, I. E., Venkatasubramanian, S., Scheidegger, C., & Friedler, S. (2020). Problems with Shapley-value-based explanations as feature importance measures. *ICML 2020*, PMLR 119.** — mathematical problems arise when Shapley values are used as feature importance; fixes require causal reasoning; Shapley values are not a natural fit for human-centric explanation goals. **Relation:** pitfall — avoid causal language ("X caused adoption") in LLM narratives. **[V]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11097v2); [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2020/poster/6519)
- **Bilodeau, B., Jaques, N., Koh, P. W., & Kim, B. (2024). Impossibility theorems for feature attribution. *PNAS*, 121(2), e2304406120.** — complete and linear attribution methods (SHAP, Integrated Gradients) can provably fail to beat random guessing at inferring local model behavior for moderately rich model classes; define the end-task concretely; repeated model evaluation can outperform attribution. **Relation:** adopt — complement SHAP with direct "what-if" model evaluations (e.g., "if one more neighboring council adopted, p rises from 4% to 7%"), which are exact for our model and easy to verify. **[V]** — [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11870v3)
- **Rudin, C. (2019). Stop explaining black box machine learning models for high stakes decisions and use interpretable models instead. *Nature Machine Intelligence*, 1(5), 206–215.** — post-hoc explanations of black boxes can mislead; prefer inherently interpretable models where performance is comparable. **Relation:** differentiation option — if LightGBM ≈ LR in AUC (likely, given Kapoor's civil-war finding), present the calibrated LR / discrete-time hazard logit as the primary explanatory model (coefficients and exact per-feature contributions), with LightGBM+TreeSHAP as a robustness check. **[V]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10154)

### Inferences
- Concrete explanation-validity tests for J2: (1) *Deletion/perturbation faithfulness*: set the top-attributed feature group to its background mean and confirm the predicted probability moves by approximately the attributed amount (exact for interventional SHAP additivity at the margin scale; check on probability scale); (2) *Stability*: rank correlation of global SHAP importances across rolling origins; (3) *Sign sanity*: monotone relationships expected by diffusion theory (more prior adopters → higher hazard) checked via SHAP dependence plots; (4) *Type split*: separate attributions for top-down mandated vs horizontal issues, expecting e.g. central-government signals to dominate the former.
- For the LR / discrete-time logit baseline, exact additive contributions are β_j·(x_j − x̄_j) on the log-odds scale (linear SHAP under independence), which are trivially faithful — a strong argument for reporting them alongside LightGBM.

- Event-history framing: the (issue, council not yet adopting, 6-month window) design is a pooled discrete-time hazard model; the classic policy-diffusion EHA literature (Berry & Berry 1990) gives the interpretive vocabulary (internal determinants vs regional diffusion) that matches the "top-down vs horizontal" split — **citation [U]**, see Gaps.

## Q6. Calibrated forecasts combined with LLM-generated, evidence-cited explanations; trustworthy-AI framing

### Takeaway
The closest published pattern is "LLM as narrator of an existing XAI explanation" (Explingo, SHAPstories), which limits the LLM's room to introduce errors; but plausibility and persuasiveness are not faithfulness (Turpin et al.), and citation quality of generative systems is empirically weak (≈50% of generated statements lack full citation support). So the explanation layer must be evaluated with explicit citation precision/recall and narrative-accuracy checks. I found no published system that combines rolling-origin-calibrated rare-event forecasts, empirically defined verbal grades, and LLM narratives with verified source citations — this combination is the project's differentiation.

## Q7 (Synthesis). (a) Recommended J2 evaluation protocol, (b) defining and validating verbal grades with ~300 events per fold, (c) wow-point framing

### Takeaway
Logical validity for J2 can be demonstrated as a chain of falsifiable claims: (1) no information after each origin enters any learned component (leakage audit card, Kapoor/REFORMS); (2) probabilities are calibrated out-of-time (Murphy/CORP/weak-calibration metrics); (3) verbal grades are empirically defined frequencies with CIs; (4) each explanation is faithful to the model and every sentence is backed by a pre-origin minute; (5) the LLM is a narrator, never an oracle, so LLM look-ahead bias cannot affect the forecast.

### Cited Findings
- Rolling-origin with recalibration and multiple test periods is the recommended out-of-sample design ([Tashman 2000](https://iaorifors.com/paper/33593)); repeated out-of-sample holdout is most accurate for non-stationary data ([Cerqueira et al. 2020](https://web.cs.dal.ca/~ltorgo/publication/2020_ctm20/)).
- Leakage taxonomy and model info sheet ([Kapoor & Narayanan 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856)); checklist ([REFORMS 2024](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/reforms-consensus-based-recommendations-for-machine-learning-based-science/)); logical leakage and multi-period backtests with Brier + calibration + log score ([Paleka et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)).
- Calibration assessment: weak calibration with intercept/slope; flexible curves need ≥200 events/≥200 non-events ([Van Calster et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6912996/)); 177 events suffice for a precise calibration slope at 1.8% prevalence in Riley's example ([Riley et al. 2021](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/handle/1874/455777)); CORP diagrams ([Dimitriadis et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7923594)); bias-corrected decomposition ([Ferro & Fricker 2012](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/8503)).
- Words must be paired with numbers in-text ([Wintle et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6469752/)); coarsening into words loses accuracy ([Friedman et al. 2018](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/value-precision-probability-assessment-evidence-large-scale-geopolitical-forecasting)).
- Unknown-at-submission forecasts as the gold standard ([ForecastBench](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea74e45a229dac70b5b63b28d8934db6-Abstract-Conference.html); [Paleka et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)).

### Inferences

**(a) Recommended evaluation protocol for J2 (concrete steps)**
1. *Freeze the target and resolution rule first* (written before seeing test results): what counts as "adopts/discusses" (e.g., first agenda item/ordinance/5-minute-speech classified to the issue above a fixed threshold), the 6-month window, the risk set (pairs not yet adopted at origin, issue known at origin), and exclusions. Record in a Kapoor-style model info sheet + REFORMS checklist.
2. *Origins*: every 6 months (e.g., 2020-12-31 … 2025-06-30 for backtests; 2025-12-31 for the live forecast), horizon (t, t+6m]; non-overlapping test windows.
3. *Per-origin learn–predict separation*: rebuild issue list/topic model/dictionaries, vocabularies/IDF, scalers, feature selection, hyperparameters, calibrator, and grade thresholds using only minutes dated ≤ t; training labels only from windows fully observed by t (purge training origins > t−6m).
4. *Baselines ladder*: climatology (pre-origin base rate) → simple heuristic (e.g., number of prior adopters) → logistic regression/discrete-time hazard logit → LightGBM. Report whether each step adds skill (Kapoor civil-war lesson).
5. *Calibration step*: out-of-time Platt or beta (or intercept-only) calibration fitted on the latest fully observed pre-origin window; no resampling; if class weights used, intercept correction.
6. *Metrics per origin* (with bootstrap CIs clustered by issue): Brier & Brier Skill Score vs pre-origin climatology, log loss, AUC, PR-AUC/precision@k, O/E, calibration intercept & slope. *Pooled*: CORP reliability diagram with bands (or SmoothECE), bias-corrected Murphy decomposition (REL/RES/UNC). Do not use Hosmer–Lemeshow or equal-width binned ECE.
7. *Type-specific evaluation*: repeat metrics and reliability for top-down mandated vs horizontal-diffusion issues; show the logic differs (e.g., what-if response to neighbor adoption should matter more for horizontal issues).
8. *Leakage canary*: rerun with full-corpus issue list/topic model; report AUC/Brier inflation as evidence that the frozen-per-origin design matters.
9. *Explanation validity*: interventional TreeSHAP with pre-origin background, grouped correlated features, perturbation faithfulness test, cross-origin stability; LR exact contributions as cross-check; what-if deltas.
10. *LLM audit*: citation existence/date ≤ origin (automatic, target 100%), numeric/sign consistency with model JSON (automatic), citation support recall/precision on a human-checked sample (Liu et al./ALCE metrics), masked-name ablation on backtest explanations (Glasserman & Lin); state that Qwen2.5 (released 2024-09-19) predates the 2025-12-31 reference date.
11. *Pre-registration*: publish SHA-256 hash of the 2026-H1 forecast file and model artifacts; describe how anyone can score it once 2026 minutes are available.

**(b) Defining and validating verbal grades with ~300 events per fold**
- *Define thresholds leakage-free*: either (i) fixed a priori multiples of the pre-origin base rate (e.g., H: p ≥ 5×base; M: 2–5×base; L: < 2×base), or (ii) thresholds chosen on pre-origin calibration data only. Option (i) is simplest to defend and stable across folds.
- *State each grade's meaning as a frequency claim*: "H = in backtests, X% [95% Wilson CI] of H-graded pairs adopted within 6 months (≈k× base rate)." Print the bracketed numbers wherever the word appears (Wintle). Avoid IPCC/ICD words ("likely") because H-grade frequencies will be ~5–20%, far below those lexicons' meanings.
- *Expected precision (illustrative calculation, assumptions not data)*: with ~300 events at 1.4%, a fold has ~21,000 pairs. If H covers ~2% of pairs (~430) with a 10% hit rate (~43 events), the per-fold 95% Wilson CI is ≈7.5–13.2%; pooled over 8 folds (~3,400 pairs, ~340 events) ≈9.0–11.0%. If M covers the next ~10% (~2,100 pairs) at 3% (~64 events), per-fold CI ≈2.3–3.7%. So per-fold grade ordering is testable, and pooled bands are tight. Aim for ≥~30–50 events in H per fold; otherwise widen H or pool.
- *Validation criteria (pre-specified)*: (1) monotonic ordering H > M > L in pooled data with non-overlapping CIs and in most individual folds (report count k/K); (2) each grade's pooled observed frequency lies within its stated band (moderate calibration at the grade level, Van Calster 2016); (3) weak calibration of the underlying probabilities per fold (intercept ≈ 0, slope ≈ 1 with CIs; feasible since ~300 > 177 events, Riley); (4) flexible reliability curves only pooled (≥200 events/non-events rule, Van Calster 2019); (5) report information loss from coarsening (Brier of probabilities vs Brier of grade means; Friedman).
- *Separate axis for evidence strength*: optionally add an IPCC-style "confidence" tag reflecting evidence quantity (e.g., number of cited minutes, data coverage), explicitly labelled as non-probabilistic (Mastrandrea et al.).

**(c) Wow-point framing**
- **"Narrator, not oracle" (LLM은 예언자가 아니라 해설자)**: probabilities come only from a model trained on pre-origin data; the local LLM merely verbalizes, with every sentence bound to a dated minute — eliminating LLM look-ahead bias by architecture rather than by hope (Sarkar & Vafa; Lopez-Lira et al.; Paleka et al.).
- **"Words that mean numbers" (말이 숫자를 뜻하는 등급)**: unlike IPCC/ICD lexicons that assign meanings by convention, our grades' meanings are *measured* backtest frequencies with CIs — a calibrated-language standard designed for rare events.
- **"Forecast receipt" (예측 영수증)** for each (issue, council): calibrated probability, grade [frequency, lift], top drivers (SHAP/what-if), cited minutes (ID/date/speaker), and the model's track record — i.e., every claim is checkable.
- **"Leakage audit card" (누출 감사표)**: Kapoor's eight leakage types with how each is blocked + a leakage-canary experiment showing what would have happened otherwise (civil-war-style inflation).
- **"Falsifiable by design"**: a hashed, pre-registered 2026-H1 forecast that anyone can score later (ForecastBench principle).
- **Trustworthy AI alignment**: explicit mapping to NIST AI RMF characteristics and to the trust/explainability goals of Korea's AI Basic Act (in force 2026-01-22), using only public data and a local open model.
