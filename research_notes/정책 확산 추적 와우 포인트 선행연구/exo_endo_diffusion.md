## Q1. Galton's problem, vertical vs horizontal diffusion, and Korean ordinance-diffusion studies

### Takeaway
"Galton's problem" — the risk of mistaking correlated responses to a common shock (or shared traits) for interdependence — is the canonical framing for our problem, and the US/Swiss literature already separates *vertical* (federal/state→local "coercion"/national intervention) from *horizontal* (learning, competition, imitation) channels, mainly with event-history analysis (EHA) of single policies. Korean work (박나라·김정숙 2018; Kim 2013; 정다정·유두호 2021; 이정철·허만형 2016) finds both vertical and horizontal effects in ordinance diffusion, but uses single-policy EHA on 기초 governments, not pooled multi-issue text+timing classification for the 17 광역 councils. The requested "하혜영·이정진 2011" could **not** be verified; a 2010 conference paper by the same pair exists.

### Inferences
- The standard literature treats "vertical" as one *covariate* inside a single-policy EHA. Our contribution is different in kind: an **issue-level classification** of hundreds of ordinance cascades, used to *re-rank* "most diffused issues" for the National Assembly. That's a descriptive diagnostic, not a causal estimate, so it fits the competition's no-causal-claims rule.
- Karch (2006) and Kim (2013) imply a **three/four-way typology**, not a binary: (1) *mandated top-down* (법령 위임/필수 자치법규, synchronous); (2) *enabled top-down + horizontal follow-on* (law permits/encourages; staggered adoption with inter-council references); (3) *pure horizontal* (no new-law trigger, staggered, references to other regions); (4) *single-region/idiosyncratic*.
- 법제처's 필수 자치법규 list is a potential **external ground-truth label set** for class (1). We could use it to report precision/recall of the text+timing classifier, which validates without causal claims.

## Q2. Network/cascade methods (Myers et al.; Hawkes; NetInf; Desmarais–Harden–Boehmke) and suitability for ~17 nodes pooled over hundreds of cascades

### Takeaway
Three families exist. (i) **External-vs-network decomposition** (Myers, Zhu & Leskovec 2012) estimates the share of adoptions due to outside sources: about 29% on Twitter. (ii) **Hawkes processes** split background (exogenous) intensity from self/mutual excitation; the branching ratio is the endogenous share. (iii) **Latent network inference from many cascades** includes NetInf (KDD 2010), Desmarais et al. (APSR 2015), SPID/NetworkInference (2020), and NEHA (JOP 2023). Network inference is the most proven for few nodes × many cascades (50 states × 187–728 policies). Full multivariate Hawkes with 17×17 excitation is over-parameterized for ~1–3k adoption events and biased upward when the exogenous background is non-stationary (Filimonov & Sornette 2015), so it needs a structured/low-rank parameterization and law-anchored time-varying baselines.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Myers, S. A., Zhu, C., & Leskovec, J. (2012). "Information Diffusion and External Influence in Networks." *Proc. KDD 2012* (Beijing, Aug 12–16). arXiv:1206.1331.** Model in which "information can reach a node via the links of the social network or through the influence of external sources"; on a complete one-month Twitter trace, **"only about 71% of the information volume in Twitter can be attributed to network diffusion, and the remaining 29% is due to external events"**; information "jumps" across the network, which is evidence of unobserved external influence. Relation: **THREAT (for "quantify the share of exogenous vs endogenous adoption")**, **ADOPT** (conceptual model: per-node hazard = external hazard(t) + Σ exposures × internal hazard). **DIFF**: they infer the external share purely from timing on a known follower graph. ### Inferences (small-sample suitability for 17 councils × 240–600 cascades × 24 months)
- **NetInf / NetworkInference: feasible, best tested.** 17 nodes means 272 possible directed edges. 240–600 cascades gives a *higher* cascades-per-possible-edge ratio than Desmarais et al. (50 states, 2,450 possible edges, 187 policies). Caveat: NetInf explains *all* co-timing via edges, so **running it on law-triggered cascades will create spurious edges** from councils with early session calendars to late ones. Run it (a) only on cascades classified non-synchronous, or (b) on all cascades with a virtual "national law" node as an allowed parent. Report both versions.
- **Pooled discrete-time EHA / NEHA-style hazard: feasible and most defensible.** Use council-issue-month rows (17 × N_issues × 24 ≈ 98k–245k rows; many fewer after adoption/censoring), with a law-shock term (months since the cited law's promulgation/enforcement), a "prior adopters" term (count or inferred-network-weighted), council fixed effects, and calendar-month effects. The exogenous term's attributable fraction gives the "exogenous share."
- **Multivariate Hawkes: only in constrained form.** A free 17×17 excitation matrix (289 parameters + 17 baselines + kernel) is risky with ~1–3k events, and (per Filimonov & Sornette) biased when the baseline isn't stationary. Recommended: (i) a *univariate-across-councils* (homogeneous excitation) Hawkes per cascade-class with an **issue-specific time-varying baseline anchored at law dates**, pooled over cascades; (ii) a low-rank or covariate-parameterized excitation (e.g., α_ij = α·f(same region, past reference)) for the network version. The branching ratio from (i) then directly answers "what share of adoptions are endogenous."
- **Myers-style external/internal decomposition** is conceptually identical to (i) and gives a citable analog figure (29% external on Twitter) for our "X% of local-ordinance adoptions are echoes of national law."

### Gaps
- Did not verify NetInf's handling of external ("ε-edge") infections or Myers et al.'s exact estimation procedure in full text.
- Found only one Hawkes application to policy adoption (Nickel & Le, as a demo). No political-science paper using Hawkes branching ratios for vertical-vs-horizontal policy diffusion was found. That's a possible novelty angle, but absence of evidence is not exhaustive.
- Stochastic-declustering / "triggering probability" methods (from seismology ETAS) that assign each event a probability of being background vs triggered were not verified in this session. If used, cite after checking (e.g., Zhuang, Ogata & Vere-Jones 2002, **[UNV]**).

## Q3. Combining text evidence with timing to label diffusion mechanism (learning/competition/imitation/coercion)

### Takeaway
Text has been used in diffusion research mainly as (a) **bill-text reuse** to measure policy similarity and copying (Linder et al. 2020) and (b) **media framing** of issues (Gilardi, Shipan & Wüest 2021). Recent Chinese studies (2025) relate provincial *textual reproduction* of central documents to adoption speed. I found **no** published work that labels each cascade's mechanism by combining *explicit legal-citation evidence*, *explicit references to peer jurisdictions in deliberation records*, and *synchrony tests*. That combination, applied to council minutes, is the most defensible novelty claim.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Linder, F., Desmarais, B., Burgess, M., & Giraudy, E. (2020). "Text as Policy: Measuring Policy Similarity through Bill Text Reuse." *Policy Studies Journal* 48(2): 546–574. DOI 10.1111/psj.12257.** Adapts a text-reuse algorithm (500,000 state bills). Validates it by showing that ideologically similar sponsors and NCSL same-policy bills have high reuse, and that **"rates of text reuse between states correlate with policy diffusion network ties between states."** Relation: **THREAT/ADOPT**. It connects text and network, but the network is inferred from timing; it doesn't classify vertical vs horizontal. **ADOPT** local alignment / n-gram reuse to separate "common template" (top-down: all adopters similar to a ministry 표준조례안 or the law text) from "copy of a specific earlier adopter" (horizontal). — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10121488); [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/text-as-policy-measuring-policy-similarity-through-bill-text-reus/)
- **[V] Gilardi, F., Shipan, C. R., & Wüest, B. (2021). "Policy Diffusion: The Issue-Definition Stage." *American Journal of Political Science* 65(1): 21–35. DOI 10.1111/ajps.12521.** Uses 52,000+ newspaper paragraphs on smoking restrictions (49 states, 1996–2013) and topic models. **Frames about concrete implications are predicted by prior adoptions in other states; normative frames are not.** Relation: **ADOPT**. It shows that diffusion is visible in *discourse* before or alongside adoption, which supports using council-minutes discourse (not just enactments) as evidence of horizontal learning. **DIFF**: no vertical/common-shock separation. — [Deep Blue](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/166196?show=full); [author PDF](https://www.fabriziogilardi.org/resources/papers/policy-diffusion-issue-definition.pdf)
- **[PV] Li, Zhou & Liu (2025), "Top-down pressure and bottom-up responses: a study on provincial Governments' adoption and textual reproduction of sports industry policies in China"** (Frontiers journal per search summary; Nov 2025). Measures the variation in 31 provinces' textual reproduction of central sports-industry policies using word embeddings / Word Mover's Distance, and relates it to adoption speed. Full text could not be fetched (PMC bot-check). Relation: **THREAT (text + timing + top-down)**. **DIFF**: one policy, reproduction of a known central text, no peer-reference network. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12672445/)
- **[PV] Li, Liu, Lu & Zhou (2025), "Passive compliance or active innovation: The diffusion of public sport policies in China." *PLOS ONE*** (Apr 2025 per summary). Grey correlation analysis on 31 provinces; **central-government pressure and peer learning** emerge as the strongest drivers of provincial text reproduction. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11967932/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0320651.html)
- **[V] Shipan & Volden (2008)** (see Q1): mechanism signatures include *coercion by state governments*, and imitation being short-lived. These give priors on how timing patterns map to mechanisms. — [Batten](https://batten.virginia.edu/mechanisms-policy-diffusion)
- **[V] 법제처 필수 자치법규 점검** (see Q1) and **[V] 행안부 표준조례 안내서** (news): Korean top-down channels leave **textual fingerprints**, e.g., "「○○법」 제○조에 따라", "법 개정에 따른 후속조치", "표준조례안". — [뉴스서울](https://newsseoul.co.kr/news/view/1065579018206707); [주간경향](https://weekly.khan.co.kr/article/202306161149011)

### Inferences (operationalization for our data; local open models)
- **Vertical evidence (V-score)** per adoption: from 제안이유 / 검토보고서 / committee minutes, extract (i) a citation of a *specific* law with a *recent* promulgation/enforcement date and "위임/따라/후속조치/시행에 따라" language; (ii) a ministry guideline or 표준조례안 mention; (iii) membership in 법제처's 필수 자치법규 list (if obtainable). Key nuance: **almost every ordinance cites a legal basis**. The signal is a *triggering* citation (a recent law change + 후속조치 language), not the mere presence of a 근거법. A local LLM should classify "trigger" vs "basis" citations, and a hand-coded sample should validate it.
- **Horizontal evidence (H-score)**: explicit mentions of other 시·도 or councils ("서울시·경기도 등 타 시도에서 시행 중", "○○도 조례를 참고"), benchmarking visits, and "전국 최초/타 시도 사례" phrases in minutes. Plus text reuse showing high similarity to a *specific earlier adopter* beyond similarity to the common template.
- **Timing evidence (S-score)**: synchrony relative to a calendar-preserving null (Q4), and lag from the law's enforcement date.
- **Decision rule (transparent, not black-box)**: mandated-top-down if V high & S high; enabled-top-down-with-horizontal-follow-on if V present & S low & H present; horizontal if V absent & S low (with or without H); single-region if <3 adopters. Report the uncertain/conflicting cases rather than forcing labels.
- Text evidence lets us move beyond Boushey-style curve-shape inference. Timing alone can't tell a simultaneous law response from a fast horizontal "outbreak," but citation text can.

### Gaps
- No verified study (English or Korean) using **deliberation-record references to peer jurisdictions** as a learning indicator was confirmed in this session. Searches did not surface one, so this remains a candidate novelty, not a confirmed one.
- Could not verify the Chinese 2025 papers' full bibliographic details (PMC blocked).
- The exact Journal of Public Policy (2025) article on clause-level similarity in Chinese policy documents (seen only as a supplementary-file URL, S0143814X25100652) was not identified.

## Q4. Simple, defensible synchrony statistics for detecting common shocks (dispersion vs permutation null)

### Takeaway
The cleanest precedents are (a) the **shuffle test** of Anagnostopoulos, Kumar & Mahdian (KDD 2008), which randomizes adoption timestamps to test whether timing correlation exceeds what non-influence processes produce, and (b) **Bass external (p) vs internal (q) coefficients** (Boushey 2012). With ≤17 adopters per cascade, a nonparametric **calendar-preserving permutation test on adoption-time dispersion** is more robust than fitting Bass curves per issue.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Anagnostopoulos, A., Kumar, R., & Mahdian, M. (2008). "Influence and Correlation in Social Networks." *Proc. KDD 2008*, pp. 7–15. DOI 10.1145/1401890.1401897.** Models non-influence sources of correlation (homophily, unobserved confounders) and proposes two simple tests using action time series. A related patent describes a **shuffle test** and **edge-reversal test**. On Flickr tagging, the authors find significant social correlation that they conclude **cannot be attributed to influence**. Relation: **ADOPT**. A timestamp-permutation null is an accepted, citable device. Our variant tests for *common shock* (excess synchrony) rather than influence. — [PDF](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~xyan/classes/CS290D-2009spring/reviews/p7-anagnostopoulos.pdf); [Sapienza record](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/332226)
- **[V] Boushey (2012)** — Bass mixed-influence model separates external (innovation) vs internal (imitation) influence for 81 innovations; "nearly immediate" diffusion is linked to common exogenous shocks. — [Pollux](https://pollux-fid.de/r/sw-2ad1a0345ab5cee8)
- **[V] Crane & Sornette (2008)** — exogenous vs endogenous bursts are distinguished by the activity profile before/after the peak. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2572957)

### Inferences (proposed statistics — our design, not taken from a single source)
1. **Dispersion statistic per cascade c**: D_c = IQR (or MAD) of adoption months among adopters; alternatively **W_c(w)** = max share of adopters falling in any w-month window (w = 3 or 6).
2. **Calendar-preserving permutation null**: for each adopter i in cascade c, redraw its adoption month from council i's *own* empirical distribution of ordinance-passing months across all issues (preserves each council's 정례회/임시회 rhythm and productivity), holding the adopter set fixed. Repeat 10k times and take p_c = P(D_null ≤ D_obs). Low p means "more synchronous than calendars alone produce," which points to a common shock. Control false discovery across 240–600 cascades (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg; standard, not verified here).
3. **Anchor-lag statistic** (when a triggering law is cited): share of adopters within 6/12 months of the law's 시행일, compared against the same calendar-preserving null. That gives direct evidence of a "response to X."
4. **Pooled Bass (p vs q) by class** rather than per cascade, because per-cascade NLS with ≤17 points is unstable. Use it only as a robustness/visual device ("our top-down class has high p/q; horizontal class has high q/p"), echoing Boushey.
5. **Structural-virality analog**: build each cascade's most-likely parent tree with a virtual "national law" root (from NetInf or from text references). Mean distance in the tree (Goel et al.) indexes "broadcast vs chain."

### Gaps
- Didn't find a published application of a *calendar-preserving* permutation test to legislative adoption timing. The design is ours and should be described as an adaptation of the shuffle-test logic.
- Event-synchronization / event-coincidence-analysis methods from climate science were not verified this session.

## Q5. Pitfalls: left-censoring, observation-window sensitivity, meeting-calendar effects

### Takeaway
Mallinson & Jansa (SPPQ 2026; online Dec 2025) show diffusion conclusions are unstable until roughly 20 adopters are observed and depend on where in its life course a policy is captured. With at most 17 councils and a 24-month window, every cascade is "short," so claims must be pooled and phrased as descriptive. Left-censoring (adoptions before 2024), right-censoring (late-2025 starts), title-normalization errors, and session calendars can each manufacture or mask synchrony.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Mallinson, D. J., & Jansa, J. M. (2026). "When Is the Right Time to Do a Policy Diffusion Study?" *State Politics & Policy Quarterly* 26(1): 83–102 (published online 2 Dec 2025). DOI 10.1017/spq.2025.10012.** Iterative EHA on **83 SPID policies adopted by ≥42 states**, expanding the window from the first five adopters. Only **22/83 (27%)** policies show a significant neighbor effect in at least one iteration, and p-value trajectories "decline and stabilize," "rise and stabilize," or remain unstable. **Coefficients change substantially with fewer than ~20 adopters.** Diffusion studies usually avoid left-censoring by starting at first adoption, but right-censoring is common. Quote: "general conclusions about diffusion determinants should not be drawn from incomplete single-policy diffusion histories." Recommendations: state where the policy sits in its life course, limit claims to early adoption when incomplete, consider cure models, and use multilevel partial pooling (Kreitzer & Boehmke). Relation: **ADOPT (core pitfall citation)**. It directly justifies *pooling across hundreds of issues* and *descriptive (non-causal) claims*. — [Cambridge core reader](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/0C1EE5747165B5745B46F62AE93EF058/core-reader); [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/when-is-the-right-time-to-do-a-policy-diffusion-study/)
- **[V] Boehmke (2009)** — dyadic emulation estimates are inflated because early adopters mechanically "lead"; condition on opportunity to emulate. — [Iowa repository](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Policy-Emulation-or-Policy-Convergence-Potential/9983982926102771)
- **[V] Filimonov & Sornette (2015)** — regime changes in exogenous rates create spurious "criticality" (endogeneity) in Hawkes fits. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6756)
- **[V] Plümper & Neumayer (2010)** — common shocks and trends must be modeled, else spatial-dependence estimates are biased; demonstrate robustness to specification. — [LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/30746)

### Inferences (concrete to our setting)
- **Left-censoring**: if a council enacted the "same" ordinance before Jan 2024, it is invisible in 2024–25 minutes and looks like a non-adopter or a late adopter. Mitigation: look up enactment history in 자치법규정보시스템 (ELIS) / 국가법령정보센터 for each normalized title, remove pre-window adopters from the risk set, and flag cascades whose true first adopter predates the window. These are often horizontal cascades in their tail phase, which a 24-month window misreads as "few adopters."
- **Right-censoring**: cascades starting after ~mid-2025 have under 6 months of follow-up. Restrict synchrony classification to cascades with ≥6–12 months of follow-up, or report a "too early to classify" class.
- **Meeting-calendar effects**: councils pass bills only at sessions, and the November–December 정례회 is budget-heavy. Raw month-synchrony therefore partly reflects calendars, which is why the null must be calendar-preserving. Use session dates rather than calendar months where possible.
- **Election-cycle effects**: the 8th-term councils (2022–2026) were nearing the June 2026 local elections at the end of the window, which may create a late-2025 legislative surge. (This is an inference; not checked against data.)
- **Normalization errors**: generic titles ("○○ 지원 조례") can merge distinct policies and create fake cascades. Over-specific normalization splits real ones. Report sensitivity to the normalization threshold.
- **Law-citation ubiquity**: as noted in Q3, a 근거법 citation is not a trigger; misreading it would over-label top-down.
- **Mixed causation**: the classification is a *descriptive label consistent with* a mechanism, not proof. Phrase results as "synchronous, law-referenced adoption pattern" vs "staggered, peer-referenced adoption pattern," per the competition's rule against causal claims between councils.

### Gaps
- Kreitzer & Boehmke (2016, SPPQ) on pooled EHA heterogeneity is referenced by Mallinson & Jansa but was not independently verified here.
- No Korea-specific study of session-calendar effects on ordinance timing was found.

## Q6. Synthesis: (a) novelty & closest prior work, (b) feasible method recommendations, (c) wow-point framing

### Takeaway
Each ingredient has precedent: Galton's-problem framing, vertical/horizontal channels, Bass p/q tempo classes, external-vs-network share, pooled pathway networks, and text reuse. **No found work combines (i) legal-citation and peer-reference text evidence from deliberation records, (ii) calendar-aware synchrony tests, and (iii) pooled pathway networks to classify hundreds of local-ordinance cascades and re-rank "diffused issues."** No Korean 광역-council study doing this was found. Frame the contribution as an *exogeneity correction* to issue-diffusion metrics, grounded in Braun & Gilardi / Boushey / Myers et al. / Desmarais et al.

### Cited Findings
- Closest prior work by component:
  - Tempo-based classification incl. common exogenous shock: **Boushey (2012) PSJ** — [Pollux](https://pollux-fid.de/r/sw-2ad1a0345ab5cee8)
  - Share of external vs network-driven adoption: **Myers, Zhu & Leskovec (2012) KDD** (29% external on Twitter) — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1206.1331)
  - Pooled latent pathways across many policies: **Desmarais, Harden & Boehmke (2015) APSR**; **Boehmke et al. (2020) PSJ (SPID)**; **Harden et al. (2023) JOP (NEHA)** — [APSR](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/persistent-policy-pathways-inferring-diffusion-networks-in-the-american-states/1003F78E735B490161A34819F796CEF8); [SPID](https://aquila.usm.edu/fac_pubs/19108); [NEHA](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/a-diffusion-network-event-history-estimator/)
  - Text reuse ↔ diffusion ties: **Linder et al. (2020) PSJ** — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10121488)
  - Korean vertical vs horizontal ordinance diffusion: **박나라·김정숙 (2018)**; **Kim (2013) LGS** — [KCI](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002393893); [Korea Univ.](https://scholar.korea.ac.kr/handle/2021.sw.korea/102486)
  - Top-down text reproduction + adoption speed (China, 2025): **[PV]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12672445/)
- Pitfall anchors: **Mallinson & Jansa (2026) SPPQ**; **Filimonov & Sornette (2015)**; **Boehmke (2009) JOP**; **Van den Bulte & Lilien (2001) AJS** — links above.

### Inferences

**(a) Novelty assessment**
- *Novelty threats*: (1) Boushey 2012 for "fast = exogenous shock" classification; (2) Myers et al. 2012 for "exogenous share" quantification; (3) Desmarais et al. 2015 / SPID 2020 for "pooled pathway networks"; (4) Linder et al. 2020 for text-plus-network; (5) Korean vertical/horizontal EHA (박나라·김정숙 2018; Kim 2013).
- *Defensible novelty* (moderate–high, given searches done): (i) **mechanism evidence read from deliberation text**: trigger-law citations vs peer-council references in 제안이유/검토보고서/minutes, not just adoption dates; (ii) **issue-level labels** (mandated top-down / enabled + horizontal / horizontal / single-region) over 240–600 cascades rather than one policy; (iii) **calendar-preserving synchrony null** tailored to legislative sessions; (iv) **dual networks**: a *reference network* (who cites whom in minutes) vs a *precedence network* (NetInf on non-synchronous cascades), and their agreement as a validity check; (v) **policy-use framing**: correcting a "most-diffused issue" ranking that otherwise mostly reflects the National Assembly's own laws echoing back; (vi) **Korean 17-광역 scale with local open LLMs**.
- Honest caveat: novelty claims rest on web-indexed searches. A full KCI/RISS/DBpia sweep for 「조례 확산」「정책확산」「회의록 텍스트」 is advisable before final submission.

**(b) Concrete method recommendations (17 councils × 240–600 issues × 24 months, local models)**
1. **Cascade table**: issue c (normalized title cluster), council i, adoption date t_ic (session date of 본회의 의결), plus text fields (제안이유, 검토보고서 excerpts, committee/plenary minutes snippets).
2. **Text evidence extraction (local LLM + regex)**: label each adoption with V (trigger-law citation, with law name and promulgation/enforcement dates looked up from 국가법령정보센터), G (ministry guideline/표준조례안), and H (explicit mention of other 시·도/councils), each with a quoted evidence span. Hand-code 100–200 adoptions for validation and report agreement.
3. **Synchrony per cascade**: IQR/W(6) with the calendar-preserving permutation null (Q4) plus anchor-lag to the cited law's 시행일. Apply FDR control across cascades.
4. **Classification rule**: a transparent 2×2 (+ single-region) from V-evidence × synchrony, with H-evidence as a tiebreaker for the "enabled + horizontal" class. **Validate class 1 against 법제처's 필수 자치법규 list** where obtainable.
5. **Exogenous share (headline number)**: (i) simple, the share of multi-council adoptions labeled top-down; (ii) model-based, a pooled discrete-time hazard (NEHA-style) logit(h_ict) = α_i + f(months since law_c) + γ·(prior adopters_c,t−1) + month/session effects, with the attributable fraction of the law term vs the prior-adopter term. Optionally, (iii) a pooled Hawkes with law-anchored baseline and homogeneous excitation (branching ratio = endogenous share), noting the Filimonov–Sornette bias if the baseline is omitted. Show the naive (no-law-term) vs corrected estimates side by side. The gap *is* the wow.
6. **Pathway networks**: (A) **reference network** from H-mentions (directed i→j if council i's records cite council j), aggregated across issues; (B) **precedence network** via NetworkInference `netinf` on cascades *not* labeled mandated-top-down (and a version with a virtual "national" node). Compare A vs B (edge overlap / correlation; QAP permutation for significance is standard but not verified here). Report as "precedence/reference patterns," never "influence."
7. **Robustness**: vary normalization threshold, synchrony window (3/6/12 months), minimum adopters (3/4/5), exclude cascades with pre-2024 adopters, and drop each council in turn (jackknife on 17 units).
8. **Communicate the window limitation** à la Mallinson & Jansa: claims are about early-phase patterns in 2024–25 and pooled across issues, not about determinants of any single ordinance.

**(c) Wow-point framing (for judges / National Assembly Library)**
- Headline: **"가장 많이 퍼진 지역 이슈의 상당수는 '지방의 확산'이 아니라 '국회 입법의 메아리'다."** A naive frequency/diffusion ranking puts law-mandated ordinances on top (e.g., urban complex development support: 14 councils within 6 months), which tells the National Assembly about *its own* laws. After separating exogenous synchrony, the **genuinely horizontal** issues (e.g., blood-donation education, wellness tourism, AI industry support spreading over 12–18 months) surface. These are bottom-up local policy demands, which is the information a legislature actually needs (cf. "bottom-up federalism," Shipan & Volden 2006).
- Two products for the Assembly: (1) a **"메아리 지표" (echo index)**, the share and lag of local implementation of national laws (an implementation-monitoring view, complementary to 법제처's 필수 자치법규 checks); (2) a **"자생 확산 지표" (endogenous diffusion index)** ranking locally originated issues as candidate agenda items, with the observed council-to-council *precedence/reference pathways* for anticipating where an issue may appear next (prediction, not causal claims).
- Scholarly grounding sentence: "Following the Galton's-problem tradition (Braun & Gilardi 2006) and evidence that common external pushes can masquerade as contagion (Van den Bulte & Lilien 2001; Filimonov & Sornette 2015), we separate synchronous, law-referenced adoption from staggered, peer-referenced adoption, extending tempo-based typologies (Boushey 2012) and pooled pathway inference (Desmarais et al. 2015) with deliberation-text evidence."

### Gaps
- Exact numeric estimates for our data (exogenous share, network density) are not yet computed. The 14-council and 9-of-10 examples come from the user's preliminary analysis, not external sources.
- Whether 법제처's 필수 자치법규 list is downloadable at issue granularity for 2024–25 was not verified.
- A full-database KCI/RISS/DBpia search for Korean text-based ordinance-diffusion studies remains to be done to firm up the novelty claim.
