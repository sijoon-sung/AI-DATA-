## Q1. Attribution and citation evaluation: ALCE, AIS, AttrScore, AttributionBench, post-hoc vs in-generation citation, faithfulness vs correctness. Which metrics are standard and how are they computed?

### Takeaway
The standard automatic citation metrics are ALCE's **citation recall** (is each statement entailed by the concatenation of its cited passages?) and **citation precision** (is each citation non-redundant and supportive?), both computed with an NLI model. AIS (Rashkin et al.) is the standard human-annotation framework. Later work shows three things: automatic attribution judges are unreliable and don't transfer across datasets (AttributionBench; Ding et al. 2026), a "correct" citation isn't necessarily "faithful" (Wallat et al.), and generation-time and post-hoc citation trade precision against coverage (Saxena et al. 2025). Our pipeline should report ALCE-style recall and precision at claim level, add a deterministic verbatim-span check that ALCE doesn't have, and validate the NLI judge on our own Korean data.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational metrics**
- **Gao, T., Yen, H., Yu, J., & Chen, D. (2023). "Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations." EMNLP 2023, pp. 6465–6488.** ALCE is a benchmark that scores fluency (MAUVE), correctness and citation quality. On ELI5, even the best models lack complete citation support 50% of the time. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)
  - How the metrics are computed:
    - **Citation recall** is computed per statement. A statement scores 1 only if it has at least one citation and an NLI model judges that the *concatenated* cited passages entail it. The response score is the average over statements.
    - **Citation precision** is computed per citation. A citation is "irrelevant" if it can't support the statement on its own *and* removing it leaves the remaining citations still supporting the statement. Precision is 1 only when the statement has recall 1 and the citation is not irrelevant.
    - **NLI model:** TRUE, a T5-11B model fine-tuned on NLI datasets.
    - **Correctness:** EM-recall on ASQA, Recall-5 on QAMPARI, and claim recall on ELI5 (sub-claims generated by InstructGPT, then checked with TRUE).
    - **Agreement with humans:** Cohen's κ is 0.698 for citation recall and 0.525 for precision. Accuracy against human labels is 85.1% for recall and 77.6% for precision.
    - — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14627v2)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT.** Reuse the recall and precision definitions at claim level. A T5-11B NLI judge won't fit on an 8 GB GPU, so a smaller Korean NLI model is needed and its agreement with humans must be re-measured. **DIFF:** ALCE cites whole passages and has no verbatim-span existence check and no structured or temporal check.
- **Rashkin, H., Nikolaev, V., Lamm, M., Aroyo, L., Collins, M., Das, D., Petrov, S., Tomar, G. S., Turc, I., & Reitter, D. (2023). "Measuring Attribution in Natural Language Generation Models." *Computational Linguistics* 49(4): 777–840.** It defines AIS (Attributable to Identified Sources): output about the external world must be verifiable against an independent, provided source. Annotators follow a two-stage pipeline, and the framework is validated on conversational QA, summarization and table-to-text. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** as the protocol for the human-audit sample. Its table-to-text validation also makes it a precedent for attributing claims to structured data.
- **Yue, X., Wang, B., Chen, Z., Zhang, K., Su, Y., & Sun, H. (2023). "Automatic Evaluation of Attribution by Large Language Models." Findings of EMNLP 2023, pp. 4615–4635.** It defines three labels:
  - **Attributable:** the reference fully supports the answer.
  - **Extrapolatory:** the reference lacks enough information.
  - **Contradictory:** the answer contradicts the reference.

  The test sets are AttrEval-Simulation and AttrEval-GenSearch (about 250 New Bing examples). A fine-tuned **Flan-T5 at 770M** scored 59.3 and 64.5 and beat zero-shot ChatGPT (43.2 and 55.0). The 11B Flan-T5 was *not* consistently better. GPT-4 was strongest (55.6 and 85.1). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.307/); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.06311v2)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** the three-way label scheme (supported, not-enough-info, contradicted) for the NLI stage, since "extrapolatory" is the main failure mode in RAG. The evidence that small fine-tuned checkers can match or beat larger prompted LLMs supports our 8 GB design.
- **Li, Y., Yue, X., Liao, Z., & Sun, H. (2024). "AttributionBench: How Hard is Automatic Attribution Evaluation?" Findings of ACL 2024, pp. 14919–14935.** It unifies several attribution datasets into one benchmark. Even a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 reaches only about 80% macro-F1 on the binary task. Most of the 300+ errors analysed come from models' insensitivity to nuanced or fine-grained information, and from a gap between what the model sees and what human annotators had access to. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.886/)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** as the motivation for not trusting any single automatic judge, and for combining NLI with deterministic checks (span existence plus event table).

**Post-hoc vs in-generation citation; faithfulness vs correctness**
- **Gao, L., Dai, Z., Pasupat, P., Chen, A., Chaganty, A. T., Fan, Y., Zhao, V., Lao, N., Lee, H., Juan, D.-C., & Guu, K. (2023). "RARR: Researching and Revising What Language Models Say, Using Language Models." ACL 2023 (Long), pp. 16477–16508.** RARR is a post-hoc pipeline: it researches evidence for any model's output, then revises unsupported content while preserving the original text. It needs only few-shot prompting, an LLM and web search. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.910/)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** the revise-on-failure loop and the idea of measuring *preservation*, which guards against over-editing. **DIFF:** we retrieve from a closed minutes corpus with a fixed evidence bundle, not the web.
- **Slobodkin, A., Hirsch, E., Cattan, A., Schuster, T., & Dagan, I. (2024). "Attribute First, then Generate: Locally-attributable Grounded Text Generation." ACL 2024 (Long), pp. 3309–3344.** It splits generation into content selection, then sentence planning, then sentence-by-sentence generation. The selected source spans *are* the fine-grained citations. Citations were more concise, quality was kept or improved, and human verification was significantly faster. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.182)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **THREAT (closest generation-side prior work).** "Evidence bundle, then generate with span citations" is conceptually the same as attribute-first. **DIFF:** we add (i) verification of the generated output, including a structured event-table check, (ii) claim-level abstention, (iii) Korean, and (iv) forecast claims.
- **Huang, L., et al. (2024). "Learning Fine-Grained Grounded Citations for Attributed Large Language Models" (FRONT).** arXiv:2408.04568, marked as accepted to Findings of ACL 2024 (Anthology page itself not opened). It trains the model to first ground short supporting quotes, then generate from them. With Llama-2-7B it gains on average 14.21% citation quality on ALCE and beats ChatGPT. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04568v1)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **THREAT/ADOPT.** It is a precedent for quote-level grounding with a 7B model. FRONT is the main baseline that Trust-Align (below) compares against.
- **Saxena, Y., Bommireddy, R., Padia, A., & Gaur, M. (2025). "Generation-Time vs. Post-hoc Citation: A Holistic Evaluation of LLM Attribution."** arXiv:2509.21557, NeurIPS 2025 LLM Evaluation **Workshop**. Across four attribution datasets, post-hoc citation (P-Cite) achieves high coverage with competitive correctness, while generation-time citation (G-Cite) favors precision at the cost of coverage and speed. Retrieval is the main driver of quality in both. The authors recommend G-Cite for precision-critical work such as strict claim verification. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21557)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** as the justification for our hybrid: G-Cite (the generator cites spans) followed by a post-hoc verifier. Expect lower coverage, and report it explicitly.
- **Wallat, J., Heuss, M., de Rijke, M., & Anand, A. "Correctness is not Faithfulness in RAG Attributions."** arXiv:2412.18004 (Dec 2024). The TU Delft portal lists a peer-reviewed conference version titled "...in Retrieval Augmented Generation Attributions"; **ICTIR 2025 is [UNVERIFIED]**. The paper separates citation *correctness* (the document supports the statement) from *faithfulness* (the model actually relied on the document). Up to 57% of citations are post-rationalized. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18004); [TU Delft](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/correctness-is-not-faithfulness-in-retrieval-augmented-generation/)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **DIFF opportunity.** Our three stages measure correctness only. Adding a cheap counterfactual "leave-evidence-out" test (drop the cited span from the bundle and check whether the claim persists) would measure faithfulness. We found no Korean work that does this.
- **Buchmann, J., & Gurevych, I. (2026). "Citation Failure: Definition, Analysis and Efficient Mitigation." TACL 14: 1453–1481.** It separates *citation failure* (a good response with incomplete citations) from *response failure*. Its CITECONTROL benchmark shows citation failures grow with relational complexity. CITENTION combines generative, attention-based and retrieval-based citation. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.tacl-1.66/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20303)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** the citation-failure vs response-failure split in our error taxonomy, so that cases where the claim is true but the span is wrong are scored separately from false claims.
- **Ding, T., Nannapaneni, A., & De la Cruz Weinstein, J. P. (2026). "Do LLM Attribution Metrics Transfer? Auditing Retrieval-Augmented Generation Evaluation Across Datasets and Constructs."** arXiv:2606.23915, GroundLM workshop at EMNLP 2026.
  - No audited automatic scorer stays within the 95% confidence interval of the best scorer on every dataset.
  - Metric rankings invert between AttributedQA and LFQA (Kendall τ = −0.64).
  - An NLI scorer reaches AUROC 0.90 on short claims but falls to 0.53 (chance) on long-form answers.
  - An LLM judge avoids that collapse but is about 100× costlier and non-deterministic.
  - — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23915)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT.** This strongly supports (i) verifying *short atomic claims against short spans*, the regime where NLI works, and (ii) calibrating the verifier on a small labeled Korean dev set before trusting it.

### Inferences
- Standard metric set to report:
  - citation recall and precision (ALCE definition, our NLI model)
  - answer or claim correctness
  - refusal or abstention quality (Trust-Score, Q3)
  - a human AIS-style audit on a sample, with κ
- Our Stage 1 (verbatim span existence) is a *deterministic, zero-error* attribution check that none of the standard benchmarks include, because they cite at passage or document level. It is cheap and highly visible to judges.
- Faithfulness ≠ correctness (Wallat) is the main conceptual gap left in our design. A leave-evidence-out counterfactual test is an inexpensive, novel addition.

### Gaps
- The exact two-stage AIS annotation guideline lives in the CL article's appendix, which was not read in this session.
- We did not find a Korean attribution or citation-quality benchmark comparable to ALCE or AttributionBench. Searches surfaced none, but the search budget was exhausted before Korean-language queries such as "인용 정확도 벤치마크" could be run.

## Q2. Atomic claim factuality: FActScore, SAFE/LongFact, VeriScore, RARR, and 2024–2026 successors. How reliable is claim decomposition with small models?

### Takeaway
The "decompose then verify" paradigm (FActScore, SAFE, VeriScore) is the standard approach. However, several 2024–2026 papers show that scores depend heavily on *how* text is decomposed and decontextualized, and that decomposition can add noise. Distilled 8B decomposers are now competitive. For RQ3 the safest route is to **avoid free-text decomposition altogether**: have the generator emit structured atomic claims (one claim per evidence link), so decomposition error is designed out rather than measured.

### Cited Findings
- **Min, S., Krishna, K., Lyu, X., Lewis, M., Yih, W., Koh, P., Iyyer, M., Zettlemoyer, L., & Hajishirzi, H. (2023). "FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation." EMNLP 2023, pp. 12076–12100.** FActScore splits a generation into atomic facts and reports the percentage supported by a knowledge source. ChatGPT scored about 58% on biographies. An automated estimator (retrieval plus a strong LM) has an error rate below 2%. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.741/)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** "supported-claim precision" as the human-audited headline metric. **DIFF:** the knowledge source here is the cited minutes span, not Wikipedia.
- **Wei, J., Yang, C., Song, X., Lu, Y., Hu, N., Huang, J., Tran, D., Peng, D., Liu, R., Huang, D., Du, C., & Le, Q. V. (2024). "Long-form factuality in large language models." NeurIPS 2024.**
  - **SAFE** has an LLM split a response into facts and check each one via Google Search.
  - **LongFact** is a set of prompts covering 38 topics.
  - **F1@K** combines precision (the share of supported facts) with recall (supported facts relative to a preferred count K).
  - SAFE agrees with crowd annotators on 72% of about 16k facts, wins 76% of 100 disagreements, and is more than 20× cheaper than humans.
  - — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT F1@K-style recall** so that abstention can't trivially maximize precision. For example, recall = covered key events / K gold events from the event table. **DIFF:** we use no web search.
- **Song, Y., Kim, Y., & Iyyer, M. (2024). "VeriScore: Evaluating the factuality of verifiable claims in long-form text generation." Findings of EMNLP 2024**, Anthology ID 2024.findings-emnlp.552, pp. 9447–9474. The venue was confirmed from the arXiv entry and an Anthology preview; the main Anthology page wasn't opened. VeriScore extracts only *verifiable* claims, because many outputs mix verifiable and unverifiable content. It runs with closed or fine-tuned open-weight models. Humans preferred its extracted claims across 8 tasks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19276); [Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix-8040/2024.findings-emnlp.552)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** the verifiable/unverifiable split. Our **forecast** sentences are by definition not verifiable as facts. They should be typed as `forecast` and checked for *factor grounding* (each cited factor exists and is supported), not for truth. This is a differentiation point.
- **Wanner, M., Ebner, S., Jiang, Z., Dredze, M., & Van Durme, B. (2024). "A Closer Look at Claim Decomposition."** arXiv:2403.11903; venue **[UNVERIFIED]** (no venue on the arXiv page). FActScore is sensitive to the decomposition method, and errors from the decomposer are wrongly credited or blamed on the generator. The paper proposes **DecompScore** for decomposition quality and a decomposition method grounded in neo-Davidsonian semantics. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11903)
- **Wanner, M., Van Durme, B., & Dredze, M. (2024). "DnDScore: Decontextualization and Decomposition for Factuality Verification in Long-Form Text Generation."** arXiv:2412.13175; venue **[UNVERIFIED]**. Decomposition and decontextualization pull in opposite directions, and the strategy chosen changes factuality scores noticeably. The paper proposes verifying each subclaim *together with its added context*. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13175)
- **Hu, Q., Long, Q., & Wang, W. (2025). "Decomposition Dilemmas: Does Claim Decomposition Boost or Burden Fact-Checking Performance?" NAACL 2025 (Long), pp. 6313–6336.** Decomposition has inconsistent effects. The paper offers a taxonomy of decomposition errors and shows decomposition can raise accuracy but also adds noise. Its illustrative example shows FActScore producing vague sub-claims and VeriScore dropping key details. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.320); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02400v2)
- **Metropolitansky, D., & Larson, J. (2025). "Towards Effective Extraction and Evaluation of Factual Claims." ACL 2025 (Long), pp. 6996–7045.** Claim extraction is evaluated on entailment, coverage (including element-level coverage) and decontextualization. Their extractor, **Claimify**, extracts a claim only when it is confident of the interpretation, and 99% of its claims were entailed by the source. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.348); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10855v2)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** the principle of declining to extract when a claim is ambiguous, which is decomposition-level abstention.
- **Magomere, J., Kochkina, E., Mensah, S., Kaur, S., Acero, F., Oncevay, A., Smiley, C. H., Liu, X., & Veloso, M. (2026). "Distill and Align Decomposition for Enhanced Claim Verification." Findings of EACL 2026** (per the arXiv comment). It trains an 8B decomposer with SFT on teacher-distilled exemplars plus GRPO rewards for format, verifier alignment and decomposition quality. It reaches 71.75% macro-F1 on downstream verification across six settings, beating prompt-based and earlier RL baselines. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21857)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* evidence that decomposition by models of about 8B is feasible *with training*. Prompted 7B decomposition in Korean is untested in the literature we found.
- **Kartáč, I., Tovarys, J., Lango, M., & Dušek, O. (2026). "VetScore: Risk-Weighted Fact Verification for Veterinary Long-Form QA with Citations."** arXiv:2608.03675 (Aug 2026), no venue listed. It extracts claims, rates each claim's harm potential, checks each claim against its *citation excerpts*, and aggregates a risk-weighted score. It correlates highly with veterinary experts **even with small judge models**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03675)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **THREAT (closest recent domain-specific claim-vs-citation-excerpt evaluator)** and **ADOPT** (weight claim types). For us, "first observation" and "diffusion order" errors are higher-stakes than paraphrase errors. **DIFF:** VetScore has no structured or temporal consistency stage and no Korean.

### Inferences
- **Design recommendation:** generate claims *as data* (a JSON list of {claim_text, claim_type ∈ {observation, diffusion, forecast}, evidence_ids, quoted_span}), then render the Korean narrative from the verified claims. This sidesteps the decomposition-noise problem documented by Wanner, Hu and Magomere. It also makes our claim set exactly the unit of verification, which is cleaner than FActScore-style post-hoc splitting.
- If post-hoc decomposition of the vanilla-RAG baseline's free text is needed for comparison, use one fixed decomposer (Qwen2.5-7B with a Korean few-shot prompt) for *all* systems. Report a small DecompScore-style sanity check, because otherwise system comparisons are confounded by decomposition (Wanner et al.).

### Gaps
- We found no published Korean FActScore or decomposition study. Two leads were not verified:
  - a data.go.kr dataset described in search snippets as Korean atomic-fact factuality data — [data.go.kr](https://www.data.go.kr/biz/dcat/metadata/15149192.do) **[UNVERIFIED: title, license, date]**
  - a FActScore-style Korean analysis mentioned in the HyperCLOVA X technical report — [arXiv 2404.01954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01954) **[UNVERIFIED details]**
- VeriScore's released open-weight extractor and verifier checkpoints (model names, licenses) were not checked.

## Q3. Self-checking RAG (Self-RAG, CRAG) and abstention or selective answering. How is abstention evaluated (risk–coverage)?

### Takeaway
Self-RAG and CRAG are the reference "self-checking" architectures. Both critique *retrieval* or *support* with learned tokens or evaluators, but neither checks outputs against structured data. Abstention is evaluated in two ways:
- **Selective-prediction curves:** risk = error rate on answered items, coverage = fraction answered; summarized as AURC and Coverage@Accuracy (Kamath et al. 2020).
- **Grounded-refusal F1 on answerable vs unanswerable items:** for example Trust-Score (ICLR 2025).

2025–2026 results warn that small models' prompt-based abstention fails under misleading context, and that justified abstention is the weakest axis even in a Korean RAG benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **Asai, A., Wu, Z., Wang, Y., Sil, A., & Hajishirzi, H. (2024). "Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection." ICLR 2024 (Oral).**
  - Reflection tokens:
    - **Retrieve:** yes / no / continue
    - **IsRel:** relevant / irrelevant
    - **IsSup:** fully / partially / no support
    - **IsUse:** 5–1
  - The critic is a Llama-2-7B distilled from GPT-4 labels (over 90% agreement). Generators are 7B and 13B.
  - Citation precision and recall on ASQA follow ALCE.
  - — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/25f7be9694d7b32d5cc670927b8091e1-Abstract-Conference.html); [ICLR oral page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/oral/19736); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11511)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **THREAT (conceptual)** for "self-checking." **DIFF:** IsSup is a single learned judgment by the generator family. Ours is an *external* three-stage verifier, two stages of which are deterministic, plus a structured stage. Self-RAG also requires training a 7B critic, which is infeasible for us. **ADOPT** the 3-level support label (fully, partially, none).
- **Yan, S.-Q., Gu, J.-C., Zhu, Y., & Ling, Z.-H. (2024). "Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation."** arXiv:2401.15884 (v3, Oct 2024); **no peer-reviewed venue listed on arXiv [venue UNVERIFIED]**.
  - A **T5-large (~0.77B)** retrieval evaluator scores each query–document pair in [−1, 1] and triggers one of three actions:
    - **Correct:** at least one document scores above the upper threshold.
    - **Incorrect:** all documents fall below the lower threshold.
    - **Ambiguous:** everything else.
  - Thresholds are tuned per dataset, for example (0.59, −0.99) on PopQA.
  - Decompose-then-recompose splits documents into strips and keeps the top 5 above −0.5.
  - The Incorrect and Ambiguous actions trigger web search.
  - FActScore is used for the Biography task.
  - — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.15884v3)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** the lightweight-evaluator plus three-action logic at the *claim* level: support → keep; contradict → regenerate; not-enough-info → abstain ("확인할 수 없음"). **DIFF:** CRAG checks retrieval, not outputs, and uses the web.
- **Kamath, A., Jia, R., & Liang, P. (2020). "Selective Question Answering under Domain Shift." ACL 2020, pp. 5684–5696.**
  - **Coverage** = the fraction of test items the model answers. **Risk** = the error on that fraction. The risk–coverage curve is traced by sweeping a confidence threshold.
  - Metrics: **AUC** of the risk–coverage curve (↓), and **Cov@Acc=80%** and **Cov@Acc=90%** (↑).
  - A trained calibrator answers 56% of questions at 80% accuracy, versus 48% when using MaxProb.
  - — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.503/); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.09462)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** directly at claim level: risk = unsupported claims among emitted claims, coverage = emitted / candidate claims. Report AURC and Cov@Risk ≤ 5% and ≤ 10%.
- **Song, M., Sim, S. H., Bhardwaj, R., Chieu, H. L., Majumder, N., & Poria, S. (2025). "Measuring and Enhancing Trustworthiness of LLMs in RAG through Grounded Attributions and Learning to Refuse." ICLR 2025 (Oral)** per the arXiv page.
  - **Trust-Score** = ⅓(F1_GR + F1_AC + F1_GC):
    - **Grounded refusal (F1_GR):** the mean of answer-class F1 and refusal-class F1.
    - **Answer correctness (F1_AC):** computed only over gold claims that are obtainable from the documents, so parametric knowledge isn't rewarded.
    - **Grounded citations (F1_GC):** the harmonic mean of ALCE-style citation recall and precision, judged by NLI.
  - Trust-Align (DPO) improves **Qwen2.5-7B**: ASQA 62.91 → 68.28, ELI5 36.51 → 45.04.
  - But the answer rate falls (ASQA 92.09% → 59.49%; ELI5 to 21.00%), and FRONT still wins on ELI5 (46.34).
  - — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11242); [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11242v4)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT the Trust-Score decomposition** as our end-to-end metric (refusal, correctness grounded in documents, and citation). The paper also shows the coverage collapse that abstention can cause on exactly our generator model, so we must report coverage.
- **Joren, H., et al. (2025). "Sufficient Context: A New Lens on Retrieval Augmented Generation Systems." ICLR 2025** (first author confirmed; full author list not checked). It defines *sufficient context* and an autorater to classify whether context suffices. Smaller models (Llama 3.1, Mistral 3, Gemma 2) hallucinate or abstain often *even with sufficient context*. Selective generation guided by sufficient-context signals improves the fraction of correct answers among those given by 2–10%. — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/33dffa2e3d2ab74a783d1a8c292f66d9-Abstract-Conference.html); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/deeper-insights-into-retrieval-augmented-generation-the-role-of-sufficient-context/)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** a "sufficiency" signal per issue. For example, the evidence level of links and the number of independent councils could be features of the abstention score.
- **Mohri, C., & Hashimoto, T. (2024). "Language Models with Conformal Factuality Guarantees." ICML 2024, PMLR 235: 36029–36047.** It links LM correctness to conformal prediction via entailment sets. The practical result is a **back-off algorithm** that removes or generalizes claims below a calibrated threshold. It achieves 80–90% correctness guarantees while keeping most content, needs few labeled samples, and works with black-box LMs. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mohri24a.html)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** for a statistically principled "claim-level abstention" threshold. Calibrate the verifier-score threshold on a small labeled set so that P(an emitted claim is unsupported) ≤ α. This is a strong, citable "wow" element.
- **Wen, B., et al. (2025). "Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in Large Language Models." TACL 13: 529–556**, DOI 10.1162/tacl_a_00754. It frames abstention from the query, model and human-values perspectives and surveys methods, benchmarks and metrics. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.26)
- **Setiawan, Y. A. (2026). "Prompt-Based Abstention Fails Under Misleading Context: A Controlled Study of Small Frozen RAG Models."** arXiv:2608.22228; "committed to AACL-IJCNLP 2026" per the arXiv comment.
  - Three small frozen models (3.8B–8B) were tested under supportive, degraded, missing and misleading context.
  - They abstain reliably when context is missing. With misleading context they still answer 41.6% of questions, and 63% of those answers copy the planted wrong entity.
  - A generator-side conflict check cuts wrong answers to 13.3% but loses coverage.
  - An NLI verifier recovers coverage but fails when parametric memory and the misleading passage agree.
  - AURC is used.
  - — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22228)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT as a stress-test design.** Inject misleading or conflicting spans, for example a similarly named ordinance from another council, and report per-condition abstention. It also strengthens the case for a *structured* check that doesn't depend on the LM's beliefs.
- **Cho, E., Lee, Y., Kim, M., Yang, J., Kwak, Y., & Choi, E. (2026). "K-FinHallu: A Hallucination Detection Benchmark for Multi-Turn RAG in Korean Finance."** arXiv:2605.29523 (May 2026), preprint. It builds multi-turn Korean financial RAG dialogues with hallucinations injected according to an answerability-based taxonomy that includes **justified abstention**. Justified abstention is the weakest axis across all evaluated models. A fine-tuned 8B detector is competitive with frontier LLMs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29523)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **THREAT (Korean + abstention-aware RAG evaluation)** but in a different domain, and it is a *detection benchmark*, not a generate-verify-abstain pipeline. **ADOPT** its justified-abstention scoring.
- AURC methodology: Zhou et al., "population AURC" with finite-sample estimators, arXiv:2410.15361. **ICML 2025 is [UNVERIFIED]**: it was seen only in a search snippet. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15361v4)

### Inferences
- The two abstention metric families are complementary, so report both:
  - **Risk–coverage curve, AURC, and Cov@Risk ≤ α**, using the verifier's score as confidence.
  - **Grounded-refusal F1** on planted unanswerable issues: issues with no evidence, a single council only (so no "flow" can be claimed), or a future-only forecast.
- Trust-Align's Qwen2.5-7B results show that coverage can collapse when a 7B model learns to refuse. Our verifier-gated abstention should be compared against "prompted refusal" as an ablation.
- Conformal calibration (Mohri & Hashimoto) turns "we abstain when unsure" into "≤ α unsupported claims with probability ≥ 1−δ," which is persuasive for a hallucination-focused jury.

### Gaps
- No published study evaluates claim-level risk–coverage *in Korean*.
- Joren et al.'s full author list wasn't fetched.
- The Kamath paper's exact AUC numbers weren't recorded.

## Q4. Verification against structured data (tables, KBs, timelines): TabFact/FEVEROUS-style approaches applicable to checking dates and order against an event table

### Takeaway
Table- and structure-based fact verification is mature (TabFact, FEVEROUS, program-guided checking), and timeline-aware verification exists (ChronoFact). However, these systems verify *claims against tables or KBs as a fact-checking task*. We found no RAG-generation pipeline that uses a **pipeline-internal event table built from the same corpus** as a consistency oracle for generated narrative claims, combined with span and NLI checks and abstention. The closest analogue is a 2025–2026 clinical paper that checks summaries against EHR with numeric and logical tests.

### Cited Findings
- **Chen, W., Wang, H., Chen, J., Zhang, Y., Wang, H., Li, S., Zhou, X., & Wang, W. Y. (2020). "TabFact: A Large-scale Dataset for Table-based Fact Verification." ICLR 2020.** It pairs 16k Wikipedia tables with 118k statements labeled ENTAILED or REFUTED. It compares Table-BERT (linearized tables) with the Latent Program Algorithm (parse a statement into a program, then execute it on the table). Both lag far behind humans. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02164)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT the program-execution idea.** Translate temporal or diffusion claims into executable checks over the event table, e.g. `first_date(issue, council_A) < first_date(issue, council_B)`.
- **Aly, R., Guo, Z., Schlichtkrull, M., Thorne, J., Vlachos, A., Christodoulopoulos, C., Cocarascu, O., & Mittal, A. (2021). "FEVEROUS: Fact Extraction and VERification Over Unstructured and Structured information." NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks.** It has 87,026 claims with evidence from Wikipedia sentences and/or table cells, labeled supports, refutes or not-enough-info. The baseline gets both evidence and verdict right for only 18% of claims. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05707)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** the "label is correct only if evidence is also correct" scoring principle. For us, a claim passes only if span, NLI *and* event-table checks all pass.
- **Pan, L., Wu, X., Lu, X., Luu, A. T., Wang, W. Y., Kan, M.-Y., & Nakov, P. (2023). "Fact-Checking Complex Claims with Program-Guided Reasoning" (ProgramFC). ACL 2023 (Long), pp. 6981–7004.** An LLM writes reasoning programs that decompose a claim into sub-tasks, each handled by a specialized function. This is explainable and data-efficient. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.386)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **ADOPT** in a simplified, *rule-based* form, with no LLM program synthesis needed. Map claim types to fixed check templates.
- **Barik, A. M., Hsu, W., & Lee, M. L. (2025). "ChronoFact: Timeline-based Temporal Fact Verification." IJCAI 2025, pp. 8031–8039.** It extracts events from the claim and the evidence, places them on timelines, and checks both each event and the chronological order. It contributes a dataset of complex temporal claims. — [IJCAI](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/893)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **THREAT/ADOPT for the temporal-order check.** **DIFF:** ChronoFact learns the timeline alignment (flan-T5 plus Bi-LSTM, per a search snippet). Our event table is constructed upstream with council and date metadata, so order checks can be exact.
- **Zeba, M., Al Mamun, A., Tithee, K. J., Sutradhar, D., Raiaan, M. A. K., Mukta, S., Mohamed, R. E., Islam, M. R., Sebastian, Y., Hussain, M., & Azam, S. (2026). "Mitigating hallucinations in healthcare LLMs with granular fact-checking and domain-specific adaptation." *Expert Systems with Applications* 329: 132966** (arXiv:2512.16189). An LLM-independent fact-checking module validates propositions from LoRA-fine-tuned summaries against EHR records with numerical and logical tests. On 104 summaries (3,786 propositions) it reaches precision 0.890, recall 0.823 and F1 0.856. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16189)
  - *Relation to RQ3:* **THREAT (closest analogue to our Stage 3: claims checked against a structured record with deterministic tests).** **DIFF:** clinical English with no span-citation stage. The abstract doesn't mention a temporal-order check (a search snippet claimed one, which we couldn't confirm in the abstract).
- Other temporal-verification leads, seen only in search snippets and not verified:
  - **TSVer** (time-series evidence; EMNLP 2025 per Underline) **[UNVERIFIED]**
  - a temporal-robustness study (arXiv 2503.17073) **[UNVERIFIED]**
  - Evidence-Based Temporal Fact Verification, T-FEVER (arXiv 2407.15291) **[UNVERIFIED]**
  - KG-CRAFT (EACL 2026, aclanthology 2026.eacl-long.302) **[UNVERIFIED]**
  - — [ChronoFact arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14964v2)

### Inferences
- Stage 3 can be almost entirely **deterministic**: normalize Korean dates (YYYY.MM.DD, "제N회 임시회", session numbers) and council names (canonical IDs for the 17 metropolitan councils). Then evaluate a fixed library of predicates:
  - `exists(event)`
  - `date_eq`
  - `before/after`
  - `is_first_observation`
  - `diffused_from→to` with evidence level ≥ k
  - `count_councils`

  This turns TabFact/ProgramFC-style verification into exact checks with near-zero false positives. That is a key advantage in a hallucination-judged competition.
- Temporal-order errors ("A 의회가 최초", "B로 확산") are the claim type most specific to *policy diffusion*. They are not covered by span-level NLI, because each span is locally true even when the ordering is false. This is the clearest technical argument for Stage 3.

### Gaps
- No Korean table- or timeline-verification dataset was found.
- We did not find work verifying *generated forecasts* against structured factors.

## Q6. What is still open (verification with small local models, domain-specific structured verification, temporal claims)?

### Takeaway
Open problems that intersect our design:
1. Attribution judges don't transfer across datasets and collapse on long inputs.
2. Small-model abstention fails under misleading context.
3. Claim decomposition is a noisy, under-validated step.
4. Citation faithfulness (vs correctness) is rarely measured.
5. Temporal and ordering claims in generated narratives are not verified by standard RAG metrics.
6. There is no Korean citation or attribution benchmark and no Korean small grounded fact-checker.

### Cited Findings
- No attribution scorer transfers across datasets, and NLI collapses to chance on long-form inputs — [Ding et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23915).
- Small frozen RAG models (3.8B–8B) still answer 41.6% of misleading-context questions despite abstention prompting, and an NLI verifier fails when memory and the misleading passage agree — [Setiawan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22228).
- Justified abstention is the weakest axis for all models on Korean financial RAG — [Cho et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29523).
- Decomposition has inconsistent effects and adds noise — [Hu et al. NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.320). FActScore is sensitive to the decomposer — [Wanner et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11903).
- Up to 57% of citations are post-rationalized — [Wallat et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18004).
- Existing verifiers judge individual events and overlook chronological order — [ChronoFact, IJCAI 2025](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/893).
- Even fine-tuned GPT-3.5 reaches only about 80% macro-F1 on attribution — [AttributionBench](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.886/).
- Small judges can track expert ratings when verification is restricted to claim vs citation excerpt — [VetScore 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03675). This suggests that narrowing the verification unit is the key to small-model feasibility.

### Inferences
- The gap our pipeline can credibly claim to fill is **"verifiable generation for temporal-diffusion narratives in Korean under an 8 GB local budget."** Its components:
  - deterministic span existence
  - small Korean NLI at atom level (the regime where NLI works)
  - an exact structured and temporal check against an event table built upstream
  - calibrated claim-level abstention
  - typed handling of forecasts as unverifiable-but-grounded statements
- Each piece has precedent, but we found no paper combining them, and none in Korean or for legislative or council records. This absence is based on the searches run here; the Korean-language literature is under-searched (see Q5 Gaps).

### Gaps
- A search on meeting-minutes and legislative-transcript RAG verification could not be run (search budget exhausted). Other researchers' notes on parliamentary or minutes NLP should be cross-checked for closer prior work.

## Q7. Synthesis: (a) novelty assessment and closest prior work, (b) a recommended verification pipeline and metrics feasible on an 8 GB GPU in Korean, (c) wow-point framing

### Takeaway
RQ3 is **novel as a combination and application**, not as any single component. The closest prior works are listed below, and the defensible novelty is the **structured temporal-consistency stage, forecast typing, Korean small-model verification with calibrated abstention, and an error-injection stress test**.

| Prior work | Overlap with RQ3 |
|---|---|
| Attribute-First-then-Generate (ACL 2024) | evidence-first span-cited generation |
| Trust-Score/Trust-Align (ICLR 2025) | citation and refusal metrics, Qwen2.5-7B |
| Self-RAG / CRAG | self-checking |
| Conformal factuality (ICML 2024) | claim-level back-off |
| Zeba et al. 2026 | deterministic checks against structured records |
| ChronoFact (IJCAI 2025) | timeline verification |
| VetScore (2026) | domain claim-vs-excerpt verification with small judges |
| K-FinHallu (2026) | Korean RAG with justified abstention |

### Cited Findings
- Generation side: evidence-first span citations — [Slobodkin et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.182); quote-first training with 7B models — [Huang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04568v1).
- Metric side: ALCE recall and precision — [Gao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14627v2); Trust-Score — [Song et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11242v4); risk–coverage AUC and Cov@Acc — [Kamath et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.09462); F1@K — [Wei et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802).
- Abstention calibration: conformal back-off — [Mohri & Hashimoto 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mohri24a.html).
- Structured check: program and table verification — [TabFact](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02164), [ProgramFC](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.386), [FEVEROUS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05707); timeline order — [ChronoFact](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/893); deterministic record checks — [Zeba et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16189).
- Small-model feasibility: [AttrScore Flan-T5-770M](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.06311v2), [MiniCheck](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.499), [AlignScore 355M](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.634/), [VetScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03675).

### Inferences

#### (a) Novelty assessment and closest prior work
- **Closest prior work and the threat each poses:**
  1. *Attribute First, then Generate* (Slobodkin et al., ACL 2024): our "evidence bundle → span-cited generation" is the same idea. We must not claim the generation scheme itself as novel.
  2. *Trust-Score / Trust-Align* (Song et al., ICLR 2025): this already combines citation recall and precision with grounded refusal, and reports Qwen2.5-7B. We must use it as the metric baseline, not claim a new metric family.
  3. *Self-RAG* (ICLR 2024) and *CRAG*: these are "self-checking RAG." Differentiate on an *external, partly deterministic* verifier with no training of the generator.
  4. *Zeba et al. 2026 (ESWA)* and *ChronoFact (IJCAI 2025)*: structured and temporal verification precedents, but in English, clinical or open-domain settings, and not in a generate-verify-abstain loop.
  5. *VetScore (2026)* and *K-FinHallu (2026)*: closest recent domain and Korean work, but they are evaluation or benchmark artifacts, not a generation pipeline.
- **Defensible novelty claims (in order of strength):**
  1. **Event-table consistency verification of generated temporal-diffusion claims** (first observation, order, cross-council flow) as a gate in RAG generation. We found no prior combination with span and NLI checks.
  2. **Claim typing with forecast-specific verification:** observation and diffusion claims are verified for truth; forecast claims are verified for *factor grounding*, an extension of VeriScore's verifiable/unverifiable distinction.
  3. **Korean, fully local, 8 GB verification stack** with a calibrated claim-level abstention guarantee (conformal) and a reported risk–coverage curve. No Korean claim-level risk–coverage study was found.
  4. **A verbatim-span existence check** as a zero-false-positive attribution layer. It is simple, but absent from ALCE-style metrics, which work at passage level.
  5. Optional: **a citation-faithfulness probe** (leave-evidence-out counterfactual, after Wallat et al.), which is rare even in English.

#### (b) Recommended verification pipeline and metrics (8 GB GPU, Korean)
VRAM figures below are my estimates, not sourced: about 5–6 GB for a 4-bit 7B model. NLI and reranker models are about 0.3–0.6B and can run in fp16 or on CPU *sequentially* after the generator is unloaded, or by keeping the generator resident and running NLI on CPU.

1. **Generation (G-Cite, attribute-first).**
   - Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 4-bit receives the evidence bundle: event table rows, links with evidence levels, forecast factors, and candidate spans pre-selected by bge-m3 plus bge-reranker-v2-m3.
   - The output is **JSON claims**: `{id, type ∈ {first_observation, diffusion, status, forecast}, text_ko, citations:[{doc_title, date, quote}], table_refs:[event_ids]}`.
   - The narrative is rendered afterwards from the verified claims. This removes post-hoc decomposition noise (Wanner; Hu).
2. **Stage 1: span existence (deterministic).**
   - Normalize text: NFKC, whitespace, quote marks, the 「」 bracket form, and middle dots.
   - Require an exact substring match in the cited document whose title and date match the metadata. Store character offsets.
   - Near-miss fuzzy matches (for example, character similarity ≥ 0.9) are labeled `quote_drift` and fail, but are repairable by snapping to the true span and re-running.
   - **Metric: Quote Validity Rate (QVR).**
3. **Stage 2: entailment (small Korean NLI).**
   - Use KoELECTRA-base fine-tuned on KLUE-NLI, plus an optional MiniCheck-style synthetic minutes set. Use mDeBERTa-multilingual-NLI as a second opinion.
   - Premise = the cited span(s) concatenated (ALCE recall). For precision, test each citation alone and run the leave-one-out test.
   - The 3-way output follows AttrScore labels and maps to keep, abstain or regenerate.
   - Escalate to Qwen2.5-7B-as-judge only when the two NLI models disagree, and log its agreement with humans.
   - **Metrics: Citation Recall and Citation Precision (ALCE definitions).**
4. **Stage 3: structured consistency (deterministic).**
   - Extract slots (date, council, issue_id, relation such as 최초, 이후, 확산, 동시) with rules plus Qwen in JSON mode.
   - Execute predicate checks on the event table (TabFact/ProgramFC-style). For example: `first_obs(issue) == (council, date)`, `date(A) < date(B)`, `link(A→B).evidence_level ≥ claimed level`.
   - For forecasts: each cited factor exists in the factor table, and the hedging marker is present (e.g., "가능성", "전망").
   - **Metrics: Structured Consistency Rate (SCR) and Temporal-Order Accuracy.**
5. **Repair or abstain.**
   - Failing claims get at most 2 regeneration attempts with the error message in the prompt (RARR-style revise; report a preservation rate).
   - Claims that still fail are replaced by "확인할 수 없음 (근거 부족)", or dropped if non-essential.
   - The abstention threshold on the combined verifier score is **calibrated conformally** on a held-out labeled set for target risk α (e.g., 5%) (Mohri & Hashimoto).
6. **Metrics to report** for the three systems compared: template, vanilla RAG, and evidence-conditioned generation plus verification.
   - *Attribution:* QVR; Citation Recall and Precision (ALCE); Citation-failure vs response-failure breakdown (Buchmann & Gurevych).
   - *Factuality:* supported-claim precision (FActScore-style, human audit); SCR; Temporal-Order Accuracy; **coverage of key events** (recall vs the gold event table, F1@K-style) to penalize over-abstention.
   - *Abstention:* risk–coverage curve, **AURC**, **Cov@Risk ≤ 5% / 10%** (Kamath et al.); **grounded-refusal F1** on planted unanswerable issues (Trust-Score F1_GR); optionally the full **Trust-Score**.
   - *Verifier validity:* agreement of each stage with a hand-labeled set of 200–300 claims (accuracy, κ; for example two passes by the solo author a week apart, or a second annotator). This answers the transfer problem (Ding et al. 2026) and the LLM-judge weakness (Son et al.).
   - *Stress test (error injection):* perturb verified claims with these types:
     - fabricated or edited quote
     - date shift
     - council swap
     - order inversion
     - false "최초" claim
     - stance or negation flip
     - forecast without factor
     - misleading similar-ordinance span (Setiawan-style)

     Report **per-stage detection recall** and the **false-alarm rate on clean claims**. This also shows which stage catches which error type, and justifies Stage 3.
   - *Faithfulness probe (optional):* remove the cited span from the bundle and regenerate. The share of claims that persist with a different or invented citation gives a post-rationalization rate (Wallat et al.).
   - *Ablations:* without Stage 1, Stage 2 or Stage 3; prompted refusal vs verifier-gated abstention; NLI model variants.

#### (c) Wow-point framing (for a hallucination-judged jury)
- **"Every sentence clicks through to a verbatim minutes span. Every date and order is machine-checked against the event table. When it can't be confirmed, the system says so."** Show a demo where hovering a sentence highlights the exact span in the original minutes, with a green, amber or red badge for each of the 3 stages.
- **A quantified safety guarantee:** "At a calibrated risk of α = 5%, the system keeps X% of claims (coverage). Vanilla RAG at the same coverage has Y% unsupported claims." Show this as one risk–coverage chart with three curves (template, vanilla, ours).
- **A hallucination stress test:** "We planted N errors of 7 types. Stage 1 caught all fabricated quotes, Stage 3 caught Z% of order and date errors that NLI missed." This turns verification into a visible experiment rather than a claim.
- **Forecast honesty:** forecasts are labeled as forecasts, and every forecast lists its cited factors and evidence levels. This is "grounded speculation" rather than hallucinated prediction.
- **Local and reproducible:** it runs entirely on an 8 GB consumer GPU with open-licensed models (Apache-2.0 or MIT), with no commercial API. This matches the competition rules and the public-sector deployment reality of the National Assembly Library.
